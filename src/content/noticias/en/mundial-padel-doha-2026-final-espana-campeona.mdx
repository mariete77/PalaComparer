Doha decided again who runs padel, and this time the gold stayed in the same dressing room twice. **Spain beat Argentina 2-1 in the men's final** and, a few hours earlier, **won the women's final 2-0**. The fifth men's world title (1998, 2008, 2010, 2021 and 2026) arrives four years later in the same city as the last one: the 2021 edition was also played here. The women add a **tenth World Championship and a seventh in a row**.

Argentina came into the men's final with two straight crowns —2022 and 2024— and twelve titles in total, the deepest record in the sport. Spain came without Alejandro Galán, ruled out by injury three days before the tournament, and with a squad in which Aimar Goñi was playing his first senior World Championship. They came through both.

## Goñi and Lebrón: the point that turned the final

The final started where nobody expected it. **Juan Lebrón and Aimar Goñi** opened the tie against **Martín Di Nenno and Leo Augsburger**, and forty minutes in it looked done for Argentina: first set to the Albiceleste (7-5) and 4-2 up in the second.

Lebrón and Augsburger are partners on the Premier Padel tour. That Saturday they were on opposite sides of the net, and the Spaniard held his partner up exactly when the match was falling away: **5-7, 7-5, 6-2 in one hour 59 minutes**. It was Goñi's third comeback of the tournament —Sweden on Monday, Portugal on Friday, the final on Saturday— all three from a set down. Eight days earlier, the Navarran was playing his first Major semi-final.

Had Argentina closed that point, the script changed completely: **Tapia and Chingotto** were next, and nobody doubted them.

## Tapia and Chingotto pull Argentina level

And they delivered. **Agustín Tapia and Federico Chingotto** swept **Paquito Navarro and Jon Sanz 6-1, 6-2 in just 54 minutes**. It was Spain's first defeat of the entire World Championship, and it landed at the worst possible moment: 1-1 with everything resting on the third match.

## Coello and Coki Nieto: the same match as 2024, a different ending

Two years ago, **Arturo Coello and Coki Nieto** also won their match (6-1, 6-2) and it was worth nothing, because Argentina took the other two. This time they had to close, and they gave nothing away: **6-3, 6-2 against Tino Libaak and Gonzalo Alfonso in one hour 19 minutes**.

Coello arrived from winning the Kuwait Major with Tapia a week earlier. In Doha he played with Paquito, with Goñi and with Javi Leal —Galán's replacement— before pairing up again with Coki for the decisive match. The world No. 1 produced his best performance of the tournament in the one match that allowed no grey areas, and Coki, who had been shuffled between partners all week, provided the stability.

A 5-1 lead in the second set became 5-2 after one last Argentine push. The next Spanish break closed the World Championship.

## The women: seven in a row and one set to remember

The women's final was far calmer than the billing suggested. **Ari Sánchez and Andrea Ustero** won 6-3, 6-3 against Aranzazu Osoro and Martina Fassio, and **Paula Josemaría and Gemma Triay** sealed the tie with 6-2, 6-7, 6-3 over Delfi Brea and Claudia Jensen that ran past two hours. **Bea González and Claudia Fernández did not have to play**.

The set Josemaría and Triay dropped was **the only one Spain lost in the whole tournament**. And there was a subplot: Triay beat Brea, her tour partner, a week after lifting the Kuwait Major together. The tenth World Championship —the seventh in a row— is also Carolina Navarro's first as head coach.

## What Doha leaves behind

- **Goñi is the find of the tournament.** A World Championship debut, three comebacks and the first point of a final. Spain no longer debates its generational handover.
- **Rotation worked.** Without Galán, Juanjo Gutiérrez changed pairings every day: Coello played with Paquito, Goñi and Leal before closing with Coki, and Sanz was reunited with Coki, his partner until August.
- **Argentina have questions.** Stupaczuk did not play the final and Di Nenno and Augsburger had the first point two games away. The defeat forces a review of the line-ups.
- **The calendar does not forgive.** The Premier Padel tour continues this same week with the **Dubai P1**, with the same cast and less than 24 hours of celebration.
