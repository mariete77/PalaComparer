The **Rolex Shanghai Masters** already knows its first semi-final: **Alexander Zverev (1) against Alejandro Davidovich Fokina (25)**. The German dismissed **Alex de Minaur (9)** 6-4, 6-4 in 78 minutes on Thursday, while the Spaniard advanced when **Arthur Gea** stopped due to a psoas injury with Davidovich leading 4-1 in the opening set. Two quarter-finals are still to be played on Friday on Qi Zhong Tennis Center's centre court (from 2:30 PM local): **Rafael Jodar–Casper Ruud** and **Daniil Medvedev–Tommy Paul**, with Sunday's final on the horizon. In a draw that has already lost Alcaraz, Shelton and Djokovic, four contenders remain to settle the last two spots.

## Zverev's best version of the tournament

Zverev produced his most solid performance of the week against De Minaur: he saved three break points in the first set (to hold for 4-2) and two more in the second (3-3), finished with **10 aces** and won **80% of points behind his first serve** (35/44). "I felt good and I'm happy with the step up from the past couple of matches," said the German, now 9-4 up in his head-to-head with the Australian.

It is Zverev's **third Shanghai semi-final** (2018, 2019 and 2026), his first since 2019, and he reaches Saturday with the **year-end World No. 1** honours already secured, **62 wins** this season and the **10,000 ranking points** barrier broken for the first time in his career.

## 'Foki's' unexpected rest

Davidovich Fokina was leading 4-1 against Gea when the Frenchman — the man who knocked out Shelton this week — called it a day because of a psoas injury. "It would have been too dangerous for the end of the season," said Gea after retiring from the pair's first tour-level meeting. "I feel sorry for him, but I'm happy to be in the next round; I've been on court a lot of time, and my body has to recover," said the Spaniard, who now enjoys two extra days off before Saturday.

It is Davidovich's **fourth Masters 1000 semi-final**, and he arrives fresh after outlasting **Tiafoe** and **Hurkacz** in deciding-set tie-breaks. In the semi-finals he will meet Zverev, a rival he has beaten only once in seven meetings (Canada 2023).

## Friday: Jodar against history

Jodar is one match away from writing his name into the tournament's record books. Aged **20 years and one month**, the Spaniard would become the **youngest semi-finalist in Shanghai Masters history**; the current record belongs to **Tsitsipas** (21 years and 2 months, 2019). The Spaniard, who has reached the quarter-finals **coming from a set down in each of his three matches** (Tirante, Lehecka and Van de Zandschulp), is **World No. 13** — with a career-high of No. 11 —, is **41-16** in his first full season on tour, has won **9 of his last 11 matches against Top-30 opponents** and has made **four quarter-finals in his last five Masters 1000s**. He leads the Next Gen Race and, with a win on Friday, would climb to **No. 9 in the Race to Turin**, passing Djokovic and De Minaur. It would also be the third time two Spaniards feature in the Shanghai semi-finals: Lopez and Nadal (2009) and Ferrer and Lopez (2011) did it before.

On the other side of the net, **Casper Ruud**: the Norwegian has completed the set of quarter-finals at **all nine Masters 1000s of the calendar** this week and is chasing his **first hard-court Masters 1000 semi-final since 2022** (Miami and Montreal). He owns two Top-20 wins this week — Tien and a retirement from Auger-Aliassime at 5-2 — and would be the **first Scandinavian** in the Shanghai semi-finals. It will be his first meeting with Jodar.

## Medvedev, the last champion standing

The only former champion left in the draw is **Daniil Medvedev** (2019), and the Russian feels right at home in China: he is **17-5** in Shanghai and **10-1** in the country this season, with a title in Hangzhou and a semi-final in Beijing. He is chasing his **70th tour-level semi-final** — it would be his 19th at Masters 1000 level, joint ninth-most in series history with Berdych and Hewitt — and is already the fourth player, after Federer, Djokovic and Murray, to reach the quarter-finals three years in a row.

Awaiting him is **Tommy Paul**, who is chasing his **fifth Masters 1000 semi-final** and would become the third American semi-finalist in Shanghai after Korda (2023) and Fritz (2024). The World No. 18 arrives fresh from halting Bergs — Alcaraz's conqueror — 7-6(5), 6-4 and, although he is 16-33 against Top-10 opposition, he did beat Zverev in Cincinnati this year. The head-to-head favours the Russian 4-1: Paul's only win came in Rome 2024.
