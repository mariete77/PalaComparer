The **GNP Mexico Major** is under way. From 23 to 29 November, the Arena GNP Seguros in Acapulco hosts the main draw of the last Major of the season, with **2,000 FIP points** for the champions and a total purse of **€1,044,849**. And it does so, literally, in the place where the sport was born.

Because padel was not invented in a laboratory or a federation office: it was invented in the garden of a house in Acapulco.

## A backyard too small for a tennis court

The story is told by the International Padel Federation itself. The year was **1969** and **Enrique Corcuera**, a Mexican tennis fan, wanted a court at his home in **Las Brisas**, Acapulco. He had no room for a regulation one, so he did the opposite of what any purist would do: he built a smaller court — **20 metres long by 10 wide** — and enclosed it with **three-metre walls** and a tennis net in the middle.

The court was not a shrunken version of tennis; it was another game. The walls stopped being the end of the rally and became part of it: the ball could bounce off them and stay in play. Without meaning to, he had defined padel's signature feature. The first rulebook was written by his wife, **Viviana Corcuera**, and given to him as a birthday present.

Everything came out of that backyard. In the early 1970s, **Alfonso de Hohenlohe** — a friend of Corcuera who spent his summers in Acapulco — took the invention to the Costa del Sol and built the first courts at the **Marbella Club**. From Marbella it jumped to Argentina and, from there, to the rest of the world.

The Major awards **2,000 points** to the champions, twice as many as a P1, and a total purse of **€1,044,849**. The finals are on **Sunday 29**. Acapulco runs **seven hours behind** mainland Spain, so the evening session there lands in the small hours here.

## From Las Brisas to the Arena GNP Seguros

Half a century after that court, the professional tour still returns to the same port every November. The record of the two previous editions explains why this tournament has a clear owner on the men's side and a more open story on the women's:

| Year | Men | Women |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2024 | Tapia – Coello | Triay – Fernández |
| 2025 | Tapia – Coello | González – Fernández |

In **2024**, Tapia and Coello had to come from behind in the final against **Franco Stupaczuk and Miguel Yanguas**: 4-6, 6-1, 6-2. In **2025** they met Galán and Chingotto and settled it 6-4, 7-6(5). Both times, the Mexican title mathematically sealed their year-end No. 1.

On the women's side, **Bea González and Claudia Fernández** won their first Major together in 2025 — the first of Bea's career — against Triay and Brea, 6-2, 6-4.

## The last record left to break

This year the story shifts. **The No. 1 is already decided**: Tapia and Coello's lead in the Race is out of reach for Galán and Chingotto, so in Acapulco they defend a crown, not a throne. What they are chasing is something else.

They have already won **three of the four Majors of 2026** — Rome, Paris and Kuwait — and only Mexico is missing, the one only they can complete. One more title would also leave them on **fourteen titles for the season**, matching their 2024 tally. It is the last record left on the table this year, and it comes on the court that suits their game least.

## A slow, outdoor court

When they won here in 2025, the press put it bluntly: Acapulco is **not a fast court and it is outdoors**, conditions that are not kind to two players who live on acceleration. On an outdoor Major, with a higher bounce and a heavier ball, matches are decided more by control and patience than by raw pace.

## The champion defends with a new partner

The 2025 women's final cannot be repeated as it was: **Bea González and Claudia Fernández no longer play together**. Fernández partners **Martina Calvo** this season — they won the Dubai P1, their third title of the year — while Bea plays with **Paula Josemaría**. The two halves of that title arrive in Acapulco by different roads and can only meet on court.

At the top, the fight for No. 1 continues: **Triay and Brea** need only to **reach the semi-finals** to seal it, per the maths published by Premier Padel, although part of the press puts the bar at the final. The discrepancy is still unresolved, and this tournament will settle it on its own.

## How to follow it from Spain

Up to the quarter-finals, matches stream free on the **Premier Padel YouTube channel**; from then on, **Red Bull TV** depending on your country. The order of play is published every afternoon on the tour's official site.

With a **seven-hour difference**, an evening session in Acapulco is played in the small hours in Spain: the Sunday 29 finals land in the early hours of Monday. Tickets are digital only, with a weekly pass from **3,600 pesos**. Organisers expect **more than 35,000 spectators** across the week and the broadcast reaches **247 countries**.
