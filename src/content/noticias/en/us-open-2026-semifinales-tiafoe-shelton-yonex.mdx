The Tuesday night session at Arthur Ashe Stadium will go into the record books: **Ben Shelton eliminated Carlos Alcaraz in the latest-finishing match in US Open history**, a 6-7(5), 6-1, 6-3, 1-6, 7-6(10-7) marathon the Atlanta native closed with a **146 mph serve** the Spaniard couldn't return. "I'm tired. It was a war," Shelton said after the biggest win of his career — one that ends the title defence of Alcaraz, who was playing his first tournament since April after a wrist injury and vomited before the fifth set.

## Shelton's war: nine straight games and a 146 mph finish

The match swung in phases. Alcaraz took the first set in a tiebreak; that sparked the American's response, as he reeled off **nine consecutive games** to lead 6-1, 3-0. The Spaniard — 15-2 in five-set matches coming into the night — fought back like the champion he is and even led 5-3 in the deciding tiebreak after four straight points. But Shelton's 140-mph serves and a few Alcaraz errors tipped the scales. No man has defended the US Open title since Federer's five straight from 2004 to 2008: the 18-tournament streak without a repeat champion will last at least one more year.

## Friday 11: an all-American semifinal... and 100% Yonex

The other men's semifinal spot is already decided: **Frances Tiafoe**, who came back from two sets down against Alex Michelsen (5-7, 3-6, 7-5, 6-3, 7-6). The result: **there will be an American man in the US Open final on Friday**, something no American has won since Andy Roddick in 2004. And the gear detail: Tiafoe plays with the **Yonex Percept 97** and Shelton with the **Yonex EZONE 98**, so the first men's semifinal is guaranteed for the Tokyo-based brand whoever wins.

The bottom half of the draw is decided tonight: **Karen Khachanov** is already through after Belgian teenager Alexander Blockx retired with a rib injury, awaiting the winner of the night session between **Zverev and van de Zandschulp**.

## Thursday 10: a new No. 1 and an unbreakable Gauff

The women's draw serves up two blockbuster semifinals. First: **Aryna Sabalenka vs Jessica Pegula**. The two-time defending champion survived Linda Noskova in another deciding-tiebreak epic (7-6(1), 3-6, 7-6(10-7)) to reach her **sixth straight US Open semifinal**; Pegula got past Navarro in three. Second: **Elena Rybakina vs Coco Gauff**, with the Kazakh stepping on court this Wednesday as the **new world No. 1** — the 30th since 1975 — after beating Qinwen Zheng (3-6, 6-1, 6-4), with nothing left to prove in the rankings. Gauff, for her part, saved **two match points** against Mirra Andreeva before winning 2-6, 7-6(7), 6-2. If Pegula wins her semifinal, the women's final would also be an all-Yonex affair (EZONE 98 vs VCORE 100).
