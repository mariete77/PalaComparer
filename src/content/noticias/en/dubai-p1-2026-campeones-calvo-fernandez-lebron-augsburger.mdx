The third edition of the **Emaar Dubai Premier Padel P1** closed on Sunday 15 November with two brand-new champions and the feeling that the last P1 of the year was also one of the strangest tournaments of the season: no Tapia-Coello, no Galán-Chingotto, and the women's world No. 1 pair gone from the quarter-finals onwards. **Claudia Fernández and Martina Calvo** took the women's title; **Juan Lebrón and Leo Augsburger** took the men's.

## Women: the Niñas Prodigio are no longer a promise

**7-5, 6-1 against Paula Josemaría and Bea González in 1h57.** That is how Calvo and Fernández (4) beat the second seeds, their Spain team-mates seven days earlier. The first set was a run of breaks and counter-breaks in which *Perlamita* had real chances to close it out; the 7-5 came through sheer resistance, and with that set in the bag the younger pair let go: **6-1 in the second** and a third title together in just nine events.

Two details frame this pair's season. The first is age: **38 years between them**, a number that in an ever more physical tour is no longer a curiosity — it is a way of competing. The second is the calendar: they arrive in Dubai **a week after winning the World Cup with Spain in Qatar**, which means winning the most demanding final of the year against the very same players on the other side of the net.

For **Claudia Fernández** there is a neat symmetry too: **she defended the crown she won in Dubai last year alongside Bea González**, her opponent in this final. For Josemaría and Bea the blow cuts twice: they are now mathematically out of the race for world No. 1 and miss out on what would have been **their seventh title of the year**, although they keep the **No. 2 spot in the FIP ranking** and that same status in the Mexico Major.

<Callout title="The champion's quote">
"We always try to do our best on court. In my case I have to keep this level of attitude because it is key to my game," said Calvo after the final, before thanking her team and a Middle East swing that ends with silverware.
</Callout>

## Men: third time lucky

The men's final ran the opposite way to Saturday's semi-finals: **Libaak-Alfonso had won both meetings this year** against Lebrón and Augsburger — Madrid P1 in September and the Kuwait Major in October, both in the round of 16 — and arrived with nothing to lose. They took the second set (5-7), but the first had gone to a tie-break and the third was decided by an early break that the second seeds protected to the end: **7-6, 5-7, 6-3 in 2h22**.

It is the **second title for Lebrón and Augsburger as a pair** after the Brussels P2 — the only one of their five finals this season without Coello-Tapia across the net. For **Lebrón** it was the 71st final of his career, ninth on the all-time list. For **Augsburger**, the fastest possible revenge: five days after losing the World Cup final with Argentina to Spain, the Argentine closed the tournament with the point he needed most.

"We lost our way a little in the second set, I had a dip, but I never turned my back on the match," said Lebrón. Augsburger was blunter: "I took a lot of criticism after the World Cup final and I'm lucky to have a team that backed me all week."

## What Dubai leaves behind: No. 1 will be decided in Acapulco

With Ari Sánchez and Ustero out of the picture and Josemaría-Bea mathematically out, **the women's No. 1 stays in the hands of Triay and Brea**: reaching the **semi-finals of the Mexico Major** is enough to seal it for a second straight year.

On the men's side the tournament moved the Finals race more than expected. **Gonzalo Alfonso climbs into the top 16** that grants access to Barcelona; **Libaak stays 70 points short** and needed the title to get in. What is left on the calendar is short: the **Mexico Major in Acapulco, 23-29 November**, and the **Finals from 10 to 13 December at the Palau Sant Jordi**, with the top 16 of the Race. After Dubai there is no more P1 until 2027.

## The rackets: the diamonds won (and one hybrid)

Of the five finalists with a PalaComparer entry, **three play a diamond and two a hybrid**, and the split of the titles reads clearly.
