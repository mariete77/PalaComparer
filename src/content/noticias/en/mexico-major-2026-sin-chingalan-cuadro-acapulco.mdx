One day before the **GNP Mexico Major** starts in Acapulco (23-29 November), the tournament has lost its second seeds. **Alejandro Galán confirmed on Saturday 21** through his social channels that he will not play at the Arena GNP Seguros either, and **Federico Chingotto** goes with him: the pair that a month ago was the only one capable of challenging Tapia and Coello's throne has not played together since the Milano P1 in October.

"I'm sorry to announce that I will also be unable to play the Acapulco Major. Thank you to everyone following my recovery closely and to all the messages of support. I keep working to come back with the goal of competing in the Finals," the player wrote on Instagram.

## The hand that will not heal

The problem is his **right hand**, injured in Milan. According to the Spanish press he has **ligament damage in one finger with tendon displacement**, and conservative treatment has not delivered the expected result. The injury has already cost him the **Kuwait Major**, the **Doha World Championship** with Spain — which his country won without him — and the **Dubai P1**. Acapulco is the fourth event in a row on the sidelines, and surgery has been on the table without any confirmed decision.

His stated target is the next one: the **Premier Padel Finals from 10 to 13 December at the Palau Sant Jordi**, where pairs qualify through the FIP Race rather than the ranking, so his presence depends on the numbers as much as on the hand.

On the entry list published by the FIP on 19 November, Galán and Chingotto were still second with 28,152 accumulated points. The withdrawal forces the whole draw to be re-ordered: this is not just another absentee, it is the hole left by the No. 2 pair.

The big winners are **Juan Lebrón and Leo Augsburger**, who go from third to second seeds nine days after winning the Dubai P1. The move comes with a draw bonus: as second seeds they land in the **opposite half to Tapia and Coello**, so they can only meet the world No. 1s in the final. Before the withdrawal, that clash was scheduled for the semi-finals.

The other jump belongs to **Martín Di Nenno and Paquito Navarro**, who go from fifth to fourth. According to Sport, the pair goes straight into the second round and skips the first. Everything below also moves up a place: Nieto-Yanguas are fifth, Leal-Guerrero sixth, Arce-Tello seventh and González-Campagnolo eighth.

## Aguascalientes, last week's warning

The last Platinum of the year, played at **Padel 3015 The Complex (Aguascalientes)** from 15 to 21 November, left an uncomfortable picture for Acapulco's top eight: **none of the top four pairs in the Mexican draw reached the final**, and the men's final was contested by **Edu Alonso and Aimar Goñi (5)** against **Lucas Bergamini and David Gala (7)**.

All four exits, verified:

- **Nieto and Yanguas (1)**, out in the quarter-finals to **Sanyo Gutiérrez and Maxi Sánchez Blasco**, 6-2, 7-6.
- **Leal and Guerrero (2)** beat Libaak and Alfonso (6-4, 6-4) and then lost the semi-final to Bergamini and Gala after taking the first set **1-6**: 1-6, 7-6, 6-4.
- **Momo González and Campagnolo (3)**, knocked out in the quarter-finals by Bergamini and Gala (6-3, 7-5).
- **Tello and Arce (4)**, out in the quarter-finals to Alonso and Goñi (5-7, 6-3, 6-2).

Four of the Major's top eight pairs played in Mexico the week before and all four were home before Sunday. At a Major, with 2,000 points for the champions, that fragility in the second tier is the best possible news for Tapia and Coello, who arrive rested after sitting out Dubai.

## The women's No. 1 is signed in Acapulco

In the women's draw the equation has been written for weeks: **Gemma Triay and Delfi Brea** need only to **reach the semi-finals** to seal the year-end No. 1 for a second consecutive season, regardless of what their chasers do. They arrive after losing in the Dubai quarter-finals to Jensen and Icardo, so the margin exists but is no longer infinite.

Behind them the map is closed: **Ari Sánchez and Ustero** and **Paula Josemaría and Bea González** are mathematically out of it. The pair in the best form is **Claudia Fernández and Martina Calvo**, who won their third title in nine events in Dubai and reach the Major fresh from winning the World Championship with Spain.
