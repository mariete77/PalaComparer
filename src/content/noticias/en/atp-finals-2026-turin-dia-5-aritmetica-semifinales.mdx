There is a day almost nobody circles in the calendar that usually settles everything. **This Thursday 19 November, the John McEnroe Group at the Nitto ATP Finals closes its round robin** with two matches (Shelton-Jodar at 14:00, Zverev-Fils not before 20:30 local) and one semi-final place still unclaimed. Zverev is already through. So is Alcaraz, after his 6-2, 6-4 win over Medvedev on Wednesday. The rest is arithmetic.

And because arithmetic forgives nothing, here is the full map before the first ball is struck.

## The maths in one sentence

The bar-stool version: **Shelton qualifies if he wins — unless Fils beats Zverev in straight sets**. Fils qualifies in almost every other combination. And Jodar — the Spaniard who arrived in Turin just past his 20th birthday — needs the least likely combination in the tournament.

This is how the group stands before the final round:

| Player | Record | Sets | Games | Situation today |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alexander Zverev | 2-0 | 4-0 | 25-16 (61%) | Qualified and first |
| Arthur Fils | 1-1 | 3-3 | 27-25 (52%) | In his own hands if he wins in two sets |
| Ben Shelton | 1-1 | 2-3 | 23-24 (49%) | Win and wait |
| Rafael Jodar | 0-2 | 1-4 | 18-28 (39%) | Only with a 2-0 win and a 2-0 Zverev win |

<Callout title="The detail that breaks the predictions">
Jodar is out… until he isn't. He has lost both matches, yes, but **one of them went to three sets** (Fils's 4-6, 6-2, 6-2 on Tuesday), and that leaves him on 1-4 in sets — just enough to win a three-way tie at 1-2. It is scenario number 3 on the ATP's official list and it is no fantasy: it is the only route by which **the youngest debutant in the field** reaches the semi-finals.
</Callout>

## The ten doors, translated

The ATP published all ten possible scenarios. Put into plain language, they come down to three wide doors and one crack:

**1. Shelton is in if he wins (and Fils does not win in two sets).** The shortest path. If the American beats Jodar and the night match goes to three sets with a Fils win, Shelton advances as runner-up (scenario 9). If Fils wins in straight sets, the Frenchman overtakes him on sets won even if Shelton has done his job (scenarios 7 and 8).

**2. Fils is in if he wins in two sets. Full stop.** No need to look at the other match. And if he wins in three, only a two-set Shelton win knocks him out.

**3. Fils is in even if he loses**, as long as he loses in three sets and Shelton loses too (scenarios 5 and 6). That is the paradox of round-robin tennis: the Frenchman is better off losing long than losing short, because his 3-3 in sets is the group's best life jacket.

**4. Jodar is in with a 2-0 win of his own and a 2-0 Zverev win** (scenario 3). If his win comes in three sets, the place will be decided by percentage of games won (scenario 4), the only clause in the rulebook that cannot be settled in advance.

And one nuance almost nobody notices: **Zverev is playing for something too**. He wins the group in nine of the ten scenarios; only in one —a Fils win with a Shelton defeat— does he finish second and draw the Ivan Lendl Group winner, who as of today is Alcaraz. The German is not playing for his ticket, but he is playing for the draw.

## Shelton-Fils: two rackets, two ways to attack a fast court

Inalpi Arena is indoor and quick, which turns the fight for that place into a clash of profiles. Shelton brings the group's biggest weapon — a serve that has bought him free points all week — and one problem: **24 unforced errors against Zverev on Tuesday**. Fils brings the opposite: 27 games won and 25 lost, the most tennis played by anyone in the group, and proof that he can survive long matches.

Their rackets explain a good deal of each style, and both are in the catalogue with verified specs:

| Player | Racket | Head | Pattern | Strung weight | Stiffness (RA) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ben Shelton | Yonex EZONE 98 | 98 | 16x19 | 320 g | 63 |
| Arthur Fils | Babolat Pure Aero 98 | 98 | 16x20 | 323 g | 66 |
| Alexander Zverev | HEAD Gravity Tour | 98 | 16x19 | 323 g | 61 |
| Rafael Jodar | HEAD Speed MP | 100 | 16x19 | 315 g | 60 |

The reading is straightforward. **The open 16x19 of the EZONE rewards vertical acceleration**: when Shelton serves well and finds the first ball, the point is his in two shots. The trouble starts once the rally passes five — there the stiffer frame (63 RA) and the open pattern demand precision he did not have on Tuesday. **Fils's Pure Aero 98 shares the head size but not the philosophy**: 16x20, one more cross, and 66 RA. It is Babolat's recipe for spin without losing control, and on a low-bouncing court it gives him room to hit cross-court without the ball sailing long.

On the other side of the table sit two opposite paths. **Zverev plays the softest frame of the four (61 RA)**, the Gravity Tour, a control profile that lets him hit flat and deep without the court adding pace for him. And **Jodar is the only one with a 100 square-inch head**, the Speed MP: the most forgiving racket in the group, the one that pays off contact off-centre. For a 20-year-old who needs free points and who ended Tuesday with physical problems, that is no coincidence.

## The other group: on Friday, Medvedev-Tiafoe is a final

While the McEnroe Group is settled today, the Ivan Lendl Group waits for the last round with far simpler maths: **Alcaraz is already top** at 2-0 and on eleven straight wins, and **Cobolli is out** after Tiafoe's 7-6(6), 6-0 on Wednesday. The other place comes from **Medvedev-Tiafoe**: whoever wins is in.

And there is a racket clash worth noting in advance. **Medvedev uses the TFight 305S with an 18x19 pattern**, the densest in the tournament, built for flat trajectories and predictable bounces. **Tiafoe uses the Percept 97 — 97 square inches, 310 grams, 61 RA**, a thin frame of feel and wrist. On a fast court the 18x19 tends to set the tempo; the 97 tends to set the talent. Friday will tell us which one rules.
