The John McEnroe Group has a winner and the Ivan Lendl Group is one match away from having one. **Ben Shelton beat Rafael Jodar 7-6(8), 7-5** in two hours and seven minutes and **Alexander Zverev closed the round robin with a 6-4, 7-6(6) win over Arthur Fils**: the German finishes first at 3-0 with six sets won and none lost, the American goes through second. And above the results, Thursday's story in Turin was a different one: **Carlos Alcaraz underwent an MRI on his left hamstring and right knee** and cancelled his afternoon practice session.

## Shelton: two set points down and a ticket

The match was never a formality. Shelton saved **two set points** in the first-set tie-break, moved 4-2 ahead in the second, let Jodar drag him back to 5-5 and then broke again to serve it out. He finished the round robin 2-1 — he had beaten Fils in three sets on Tuesday and lost to Zverev on Wednesday — and wrote the line of the day on the camera lens: *"I'll be watching Arthur"*.

At 24 he is **the youngest American in the semi-finals of the ATP Finals since Andy Roddick in 2004**, when the Nebraskan was 22. His serve has been the group's best weapon all week; the rest of the point has been the problem, as the 24 unforced errors on Tuesday showed.

## Zverev: five match points erased and a perfect week

The German was already through and still played as if his ticket depended on it. He broke at 3-3 in the first set, served out the 6-4 comfortably and went **6-1 up** in the second-set tie-break. Then came Fils: **five consecutive match points saved** to level at six-all. Zverev took the 7-6(6) and finished the round robin with **3-0 and six sets to his name, none against**.

That perfect record pays in the draw, not just in the stats: as winner of the McEnroe Group he faces the runner-up of the Ivan Lendl Group, decided on Friday. The year-end No. 1, who collected his ATP trophy for finishing 2026 on top this week, has still not dropped a set at the Inalpi Arena.

## Two debuts that end here

**Rafael Jodar leaves Turin at 0-3**, but with a detail nobody can take away from him: the 20-year-old Spaniard, the youngest debutant in the field, pushed Shelton for two hours and seven minutes with two set points in his favour in the opening tie-break. His next appointment is already confirmed: the **Davis Cup Final 8 with Spain** in Bologna.

**Arthur Fils** also closes his first appearance with one win (Tuesday's over Jodar, **the first French victory at the event since Jo-Wilfried Tsonga in 2011**) and two defeats. At 21 he leaves knowing the place slipped away in the set he could not win against Shelton.

## Alcaraz: the leg, the MRI and a rule that allows no shortcuts

On Wednesday, after beating Medvedev 6-2, 6-4 to secure first place in the Lendl Group, Alcaraz put it plainly: **"I feel it a little bit. I'm not gonna lie."** On Thursday, according to Sky Sport Italia and EFE, he went to the Istituto di Medicina dello Sport (in at 12:08, out around 13:15) for an **MRI on his left hamstring and his right knee**, the area he had already been protecting before the tournament. His 1 pm session with Brazilian junior Guto Miguel was cancelled a minute before it was due to start.

On Thursday night, Spanish radio Cadena COPE reported that **"the medical tests went well and the man from Murcia will stay in the tournament"**, and that he is expected to play **Flavio Cobolli** on Friday at 14:00. At the time of writing, neither the ATP nor the player had issued an official statement.

And here is the detail that turns a meaningless match into a decisive one. Alcaraz-Cobolli means nothing in the standings — the Spaniard is first and the Italian is already out — but **the rule does**: a player who withdraws from a round-robin match after the first round cannot play the knockout stage. If Alcaraz does not walk on court on Friday, he is out of the tournament and there is no semi-final against Shelton. It is not a question of rest: play, or stay in the locker room.

## Friday: Medvedev-Tiafoe is worth a semi-final

The Lendl Group closes with far simpler maths than the McEnroe. **Tiafoe beat Cobolli 7-6(6), 6-0** on Wednesday to end the Italian's hopes; **Medvedev** arrives with Monday's win over Cobolli and the defeat to Alcaraz. So **Friday's Medvedev-Tiafoe decides the second place for whoever wins it, whatever the score**.

It is the style clash we have been waiting a week for, and also the clash of the two most different rackets in the field: **Medvedev with the Tecnifibre TFight 305S, an 18x19** — the densest pattern in the draw — and **Tiafoe with the Yonex Percept 97, 310 grams and 61 RA**. In plain terms: flat trajectories and predictable bounces against a thin frame built for wrist and feel. On a fast indoor court, the 18x19 usually sets the tempo; the 97 usually sets the talent.

## Four rackets between 97 and 98 square inches for two semi-finals

With the draw almost settled, here is a quirk that says a lot about how the game is played indoors today: **none of the players still alive in Turin goes above 98 square inches**. These are control frames, not forgiveness frames.

Look at the ends of the table. **Zverev and Tiafoe share stiffness (61 RA) and nothing else**: the German carries 323 grams and a 98 to hit flat and deep from the back; the American drops to 310 and a 97 to hit with wrist. **Shelton and Medvedev share 63 RA and the weight (320 g) but are separated by the pattern**: 16x19 versus 18x19, two different philosophies for attacking the same low bounce. And **Alcaraz is the stiffest of the five (66 RA)**, the only Pure Aero on the list, with a 16x20 that chases spin without giving up control.
