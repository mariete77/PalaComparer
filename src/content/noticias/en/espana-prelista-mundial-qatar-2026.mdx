On **September 16**, the **Spanish Padel Federation (FEP)** released its preliminary squad of **24 players — twelve men and twelve women** — for the **2026 World Championships**, which will be held in **Doha, Qatar, from November 2 to 7**. Head coaches **Juanjo Gutiérrez** (men) and **Carolina Navarro** (women) will cut the list down to **16 — eight per category** — at a special event on **Thursday, September 24 at 1:00 PM CET** at the **CUPRA City Garage in Madrid**, streamed live on the FEP's official YouTube channel.

Spain arrives with the usual weight of expectation: the women's team defends the title it won in Doha 2024, while the men's side will try to reclaim the crown it lost to **Argentina** in that same final two years ago.

## The twelve men in contention

Gutiérrez's shortlist features virtually all of the elite: **Arturo Coello, Ale Galán, Juan Lebrón, Paquito Navarro, Mike Yanguas, Coki Nieto, Jon Sanz, Aimar Goñi, Javi Leal, Fran Guerrero, Momo González and Pol Hernández**. Only four of them will be left out.

The boldest picks are **Aimar Goñi** — the young player who has improved the most in recent months — and **Pol Hernández**, both selected ahead of names with better world rankings. Doha will also be a test for the pairings that have been reshuffled on the pro tour.

## The twelve women in contention

Carolina Navarro, freshly installed as head coach, faces the sweetest of problems: **Gemma Triay, Ari Sánchez, Paula Josemaría, Bea González, Claudia Fernández, Andrea Ustero, Martina Calvo, Marta Ortega, Tamara Icardo, Alejandra Salazar, Marina Guinart and Alejandra Alonso**. Seven of the twelve sit inside the **world top 10**.

Two names stand out: **Andrea Ustero**, who just won her first Major in Paris alongside Ari Sánchez, and **Alejandra Salazar**, who after her farewell from the pro tour in Madrid is included in the preliminary list and could close her career with one last run for the national team.

## The absences making headlines

Spain's depth turns any squad into a headache. **Eduardo Alonso (world No. 18), Javi Garrido (No. 20) and Álex Ruiz (No. 21, silver medallist at the 2024 World Championships)** are left out of the men's twelve: Garrido's inconsistent form and Álex Ruiz's return from the glass-pane scare in San Luis are the most debated cases. On the women's side, **Jess Castello** — who played the 2024 World Cup — **Carmen Goenaga, Bea Caldera, Victoria Iglesias and Nuria Rodríguez** are also missing from the preliminary squad.

The final squad will be revealed on **Thursday, September 24 at 1:00 PM CET** at the **CUPRA City Garage in Madrid**, live on the FEP's YouTube channel. The 24 preliminary players will be reduced to the **16 — eight women and eight men** — who will represent Spain in Doha from **November 2 to 7**. Qatar has also been confirmed as host of the 2028 edition, according to the FIP.
</Callout>

## A World Cup in the middle of the decisive stretch

The World Championships land at the hottest point of the season: Premier Padel enters its final run with the **Kuwait Major** in October and the **Mexico Major** in November, ahead of the **Premier Padel Finals in Barcelona** in December. Doha sits right in the middle of that fight for points, titles and rankings — and with the women's team defending its crown and the men's side chasing revenge against Argentina, the World Cup promises to be one of the biggest chapters of the year. Madrid, meanwhile, is enjoying one of its biggest padel weeks: the **FIP World Cup Qualifiers Europe** run in the Spanish capital from **September 22 to 26**, in parallel to the countdown to the final list.
