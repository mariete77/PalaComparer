The **Saló de Cent at Barcelona City Hall** hosted the official presentation of the **Qatar Airways Premier Padel Finals** on Tuesday November 17, the event that closes the circuit's season from **December 10 to 13** at the **Palau Sant Jordi**. In front of more than 150 guests, with **Agustín Tapia**, **Ari Sánchez** and **Andrea Ustero** on stage, organisers put a goal on the table that goes beyond sport: **breaking the world record for attendance at a single day of padel**.

## The record in their sights: 16,920

The mark to beat is **16,920 spectators**, the crowd that filled **Parque Roca** for the semi-finals of the **Buenos Aires Premier Padel P1 in 2026**. To try, organisers have **increased the capacity of the Palau Sant Jordi stand**, aiming to **break the 17,000 barrier** for the first time in a single day.

| Edition | Best single day |
| --- | --- |
| WPT Finals 2023 (Palau) | 15,779 |
| Finals 2025 (Palau) | 15,201 |
| Buenos Aires P1 2026 | 16,920 |
| Finals 2026 (target) | 17,000+ |

The 2023 figure is not random: those were the last World Padel Tour Finals, and the semi-finals at the Palau set a world record of **15,779** spectators that later moved to Argentina. Barcelona wants back a throne it has already occupied.

> "This year in Barcelona we're going for the attendance record. We want the Finals to draw a bigger crowd than ever. It's a big challenge, but the goal is for it to be the best tournament we have ever organised."
>
> — **Joan Cuscó**, president of Esedos Padel

## An event no longer measured only on court

The internal bar is high too. In 2025 the tournament drew **more than 50,000 spectators across four days**, with fans from **86 countries**, and passed **four million television viewers** through a global broadcast of more than **3,000 hours**. Those are large-scale event numbers, not niche-tournament numbers, and they explain why the fight over the host city for the coming years has turned political.

Cuscó also confirmed that **very few tickets remain for Saturday and Sunday**, the semi-final and final days: anyone wanting to watch the finish in person will have to hunt the resale market or settle for television. The commercial message was blunt: "you'll see things that have never been seen before."

## Carraro wants the Finals to stay in Barcelona

International Padel Federation president **Luigi Carraro** was the one to publicly back the Catalan host city as the circuit keeps expanding internationally:

> "We all have to work together so the Finals stay in Barcelona, because it would be a victory for Spanish, Catalan and global padel."

Worth reading precisely: it is an institutional wish, **not an award**. With other cities in the frame, this edition's performance — attendance, audiences and business — will serve as the argument either to renew or to move the tournament.

<Callout title="One number to remember">
The world record for attendance at a single day of padel is **16,920 spectators** (semi-finals of the Buenos Aires P1 2026). The Palau Sant Jordi has been expanded to try to go past **17,000** on December 12 and 13.
</Callout>

## What is at stake on court

The Palau hosts **the season's top 16 men and top 16 women**, that is, **eight pairs per draw** qualified through the FIP Race. In the men's draw, **Tapia and Coello** defend the title they won in 2025 and chase something nobody has managed: **no pair has won these Finals twice** since they were created in 2024 (the first edition went to **Coki Nieto and Jon Sanz**). Tapia also arrives after two weeks of enforced rest following his withdrawal from the Dubai P1.

In the women's draw, the local angle belongs to **Ari Sánchez and Andrea Ustero**, both Catalan and one of the pairs of the year. And there is a sentimental thread: **Alejandra Salazar**, retiring at the end of this season, is playing for her ticket in Acapulco so she can sign off on court at the Sant Jordi.

## Schedule and where to watch

- **Thursday December 10 and Friday 11:** quarter-finals.
- **Saturday 12:** semi-finals, the record day.
- **Sunday 13:** women's and men's finals.

In Spain, matches stream on **YouTube (Premier Padel's official channel)** in the early rounds and on **Red Bull TV** from the quarter-finals, plus **Movistar Plus+ (channel 8)**.
