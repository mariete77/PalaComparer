The quarter-finals of the Doha World Padel Championships delivered the draw almost everyone expected: **Spain and Argentina in both semi-finals**. But they delivered something more interesting than the draw, and that is the line-ups.

At a national-team tournament the pairings are not decided by the market. They are decided by a captain with eight players and three matches per tie. The result is duos you never see on the Premier Padel tour, reunions the tour had already broken up in August, and a world No. 1 playing next to a debutant. Today, Friday 6 November, the four semi-finals are played: women from **12:00**, men from **16:00** (CET).

## Spain didn't need the third match

Spain's men's team solved the most awkward tie in the draw — Brazil — **without playing the third match**. **Coki Nieto and Jon Sanz** opened with 6-2, 6-2 and **Javi Leal and Arturo Coello** closed it out 6-2, 6-1. **Juan Lebrón and Aimar Goñi** never got on court.

The story is not the scoreline, it is who played. Coello, world No. 1 alongside Tapia on tour, competed next to **Javi Leal**, the player who came into the squad after **Ale Galán's** injury and who is playing his first senior World Championship. They won without dropping a set.

## Argentina, three out of three

The Argentinians needed all three matches against Germany and won every one: **Chingotto and Alfonso** 6-1, 6-1, **Augsburger and Di Nenno** 6-0, 6-4, and **Stupaczuk and Arce** 6-3, 6-0. No sets dropped, and the feeling that the top of the team is clicking just when it matters.

In the other two ties, **Italy** had to come from behind in both of their first two matches to beat Sweden 2-0 — the toughest tie anyone has had in these quarter-finals — and **Portugal** won by the same score against the Netherlands.

## The reunion the tour broke in August

In August, **Jon Sanz** left Coki Nieto to play with **Franco Stupaczuk** from the Madrid P1 onwards. In Doha, the line-ups have gone the other way: Sanz is back with Coki, and Stupa appears alongside **Maxi Arce**. Both won their quarter-final matches, and both are one victory from the final.

That is the beauty of this tournament: Spain and Argentina are in opposite halves of the draw, so Sanz and Stupaczuk can only meet **on Saturday**, in the final. The Spaniard admitted this week that he has pictured that match. It would be the first time they have faced each other since they went their separate ways.

## Josemaría and Bea, together at last

In the women's draw the story was not a surprise, it was a pairing. **Paula Josemaría and Bea González** are partners on tour and one of the best duos of the season, yet they had not played **a single match** together at this World Cup until the quarter-finals. They debuted against Sweden with **6-0, 6-1**.

Behind them, **Marta Ortega and Martina Calvo** won 6-0, 6-2, and Spain closed the tie without dropping a set in the entire tournament. Argentina gave Portugal no chance either: **Brea and Jensen** posted another double 6-0, **Osoro and Fassio** won 6-1, 6-3, and **Riera and Bidahorria** finished it 6-2, 6-0.

The shock of the day came on the other side of the draw: **France**, second in their group, knocked out the Netherlands 2-0, a team that had won their group with a perfect record. The French are Spain's semi-final opponents. Italy did the same to Brazil and will face Argentina.

## The world No. 3 can't play with his own partner

One image sums up this tournament better than anything else: **Federico Chingotto and Ale Galán** are the world's third-ranked pair and won the Madrid P1 this year. In Doha they cannot play together, because one is Argentinian and the other Spanish. And Galán is out injured anyway. Two men who share a professional project compete here for different nations that could only meet in a final the injured man will not play.

It is not an anomaly, it is the rule. **Augsburger and Di Nenno**, regular rivals on tour, are partners for Argentina. So are **Osoro and Fassio**. The World Cup is the only place where the ranking does not order the pairings.

## The four semi-finals

- **Spain v France** (women, 12:00, centre court). Spain have not dropped a set all tournament; France arrive after knocking out a group winner. On paper it is the most lopsided of the four ties, and that is exactly the risk.
- **Argentina v Italy** (women, 12:00, Grand Stand 1). Brea and Jensen arrive on the back of two 6-0 sets in their last match, and Italy are the team that has grown the most.
- **Argentina v Italy** (men, 16:00, centre court). The defending champions, who won three matches without dropping a set in the quarter-finals, against the side that had to come from behind twice to get here.
- **Spain v Portugal** (men, 16:00, Grand Stand 1). An Iberian derby against a Portugal side on the rise: they won their group and dispatched the Netherlands 2-0.

## What Spain are playing for

The women are defending the crown they won in 2024. The men want to win back a World Championship that ended in Argentina's hands two years ago. Saturday's two finals could again be Spain v Argentina in both draws, and that is the scenario the bracket has been pointing at for two days.

First they have to win two ties that leave no room for a bad day. And in a three-match format, with pairings thrown together in four days, anyone who switches off for forty minutes goes home.
