The **Dubai P1** quarter-finals left the draw exactly where it started: wide open. With Tapia-Coello and Galán-Chingotto already out, the last P1 of the season (Hamdan Sports Complex, 8-15 November, €479,068 and 1,000 points for the champions) reaches **this Saturday's** semi-finals with **three of the four men's pairs from outside the top eight**, one pair that came through qualifying and, in the women's draw, the world No. 1s gone early.

Today's matchups:

- **Libaak-Alfonso vs De Pascual-Garrido.**
- **Lebrón-Augsburger (2) vs Abbate-Dal Bianco.**
- **Fernández-Calvo vs Icardo-Jensen.**
- **Josemaría-Bea González vs Ari Sánchez-Ustero.**

The finals are on Sunday 15.

## Abbate and Dal Bianco: from qualifying to the semi-finals

This is the story of the tournament. **Flavio Abbate (world No. 47) and Franco Dal Bianco (No. 81)** came into the main draw through qualifying, took one of the places freed by the withdrawals at the top and on Friday **knocked out Momo González and Lucas Campagnolo, the eighth seeds, 7-6 3-6 6-2**. For both of them it is **the best result of their Premier Padel careers**.

Abbate, an Italian left-hander, had already made noise at the Madrid P1; Dal Bianco was **world No. 1 on the A1 circuit** and met **Maxi Arce**, his partner back then, at the net for one of the embraces of the day. In the semi-finals they face the highest-ranked pair left in the draw, so logic says the run ends here. Logic also said they would not get out of qualifying.

## Lebrón and Augsburger, the only top-eight pair standing

The second seeds had a harder time than expected against **Tello and Arce**, the pair who had just played the Kuwait Major final: **6-3 3-6 6-2** in a match that turned against them in the second set. Juan and Leo are **the only pair in the draw who have won a Premier Padel event this season** (the Brussels P2 in April, coming back from a set down in the final against Coello and Tapia) and, with the two best pairs in the world absent, they are also the ones with the most to lose in Dubai: if they do not lift the trophy on Sunday, the year ends without another P1.

## Stupaczuk and Sanz come up empty

On the other side, **De Pascual and Garrido** produced the other upset of the day: **6-1 7-5** against **Stupaczuk and Sanz (3)** in barely an hour and a quarter, in a match decided at the start. The pair from Pergamino and Córdoba came out at full throttle, took the first set in 25 minutes and held off the favourites' push in the second. They had already knocked out **Yanguas and Coki Nieto** in the round of 16 (7-6 3-6 7-6) and are now two wins from their first final on the tour.

Completing the draw are the pair who have looked best all week: **Libaak and Alfonso**, who came from a set down against **Bautista and Bergamini** (4-6 6-3 7-6) and have not lost a set once the match goes long.

## Women's draw: the No. 1s fall and the race reopens

The shock came first thing. **Tamara Icardo and Claudia Jensen beat Gemma Triay and Delfi Brea 6-3 3-6 6-1**, and the world No. 1s go home **without reaching a semi-final for the first time all season**.

It took no ordinary pair to do it: Icardo and Jensen are **the only pair to have beaten them in a final this year**, at the Bordeaux P2 (3-6 6-1 6-1), and they repeated the recipe — stay in the rallies, then step up when the match gets heavy. In the third set there was no contest.

The ranking effect is immediate. Triay and Brea arrived in Dubai with a **2,780-point lead over Ari Sánchez and Ustero** and a first mathematical chance to seal the year-end No. 1. With the leaders out in the quarters, **Ari and Andrea are back in the fight for real**: they beat Velasco and Eugenio without dropping a set (6-2 6-3) and are two matches from the title.

On the other side of the draw, **Bea González and Paula Josemaría** gave Guinart and Ale Alonso nothing (**6-3 6-1** in just over an hour), while **Claudia Fernández and Martina Calvo** had a real scare against Rufo and Iglesias: **7-6(2) 6-4** in almost two hours. The stat behind Gaby Reca's pair is easy to miss: **nine tournaments together, nine semi-finals**.
