The tour is back in action: the **CUPRA Rotterdam Premier Padel P2** runs **from September 28 to October 4** in the Netherlands, with qualifying underway these days and the main draw starting this week — the quarter-finals, on Friday October 2, are already scheduled on Red Bull TV, which broadcasts from that round. And the field is the full one: the tournament's official player list places **Agustín Tapia and Arturo Coello as the top seeds**, followed by **Alejandro Galán and Federico Chingotto** (02), **Leandro Augsburger and Juan Lebrón** (03), and, on the women's side, **Gemma Triay and Delfi Brea** (01), **Paula Josemaría and Bea González** (02), **Ariana Sánchez and Andrea Ustero** (03) and **Claudia Fernández and Martina Calvo** (04).

With 17 tournaments completed and seven to go, **both races for world No. 1 remain wide open** — and the four pairs at the center of each race are all in Rotterdam. These are the aspirants' rackets, all of them in our catalog.

## Leal-Guerrero arrive with the Lyon title under their arm

The fresh story of the week: **Javi Leal and Fran Guerrero were crowned FIP Platinum Lyon champions yesterday**, beating the tournament's top seeds, Franco Stupaczuk and Jon Sanz, **4-6, 6-4, 6-1** (official results in the padelfip.com draws). In Rotterdam they will be the **seventh seeds**, after being the breakout pair of the season: at the Pretoria P1 they knocked out Lebrón-Augsburger in the quarter-finals and Coello-Tapia in the semi-finals (**7-5, 7-5**), ending a run of 27 consecutive finals for the world No. 1s. In the Lyon women's event, the surprise came from **Fassio-Pérez**, champions over Caldera-Goenaga.

## The men's race: nine titles to seven

**Tapia-Coello have won nine titles in 2026** (Riyadh, Cancún, the Italy Major, Valencia, Valladolid, Bordeaux, Málaga, London and the Paris Major); **Galán-Chingotto have seven** (Gijón, Miami, NewGiza, Asunción, Buenos Aires, Pretoria and the Madrid P1). They have met in **11 finals**, with the No. 1s leading 7-4 and on a **six-match winning streak** since the Italy Major in Rome. In the FIP ranking the gap is 2,165 points (20,454 to 18,289); in the 2026 Race, which counts only this season's results, it is 1,270 — those are the 2025 points Coello-Tapia still have to drop before the Barcelona Finals, and the real target for Galán-Chingotto. One stat says it all: **Chingalán are 64-10 this year, more match wins than any other pair** on tour, but they lost them in the weeks with the biggest points on offer. Like in Paris, where they **served for the title at 5-3** in the deciding set and then lost four straight games and the tie-break 7-1: Tapia remains unbeaten at Roland Garros across the Major's four editions.

## Lebrón-Augsburger, the only pair to break the duopoly

**Juan Lebrón and Leo Augsburger are the only pair to have won a title outside the duopoly** this year: the Brussels P2 in April, against Coello-Tapia themselves, **2-6, 6-3, 6-3**. Since then they have piled up near-misses — the Cancún final (6-7, 6-3, 7-5), the Málaga final (6-2, 6-2) and the Paris semi-final (7-5, 6-3) — with a **40-16** record that keeps them fifth (8,040 points) and sixth (7,359) in the world, both already qualified for the Barcelona Finals. They are the third seeds in Rotterdam, and Lebrón's <ProductRef id="babolat-technical-viper-3-0-2026" /> is the reference racket in our catalog. ## The women: six titles apiece

The women's bracket is as tight as it gets: **Triay-Brea and Josemaría-González have six titles each**, with Triay and Brea reaching **14 of the 17 finals** this season and González-Josemaría posting the longest streak of the year (five straight tournaments from Miami to Buenos Aires). They have met in **eight finals**, 5-3 in favor of Josemaría-González, but the last two — Málaga (7-5, 3-6, 7-6) and Madrid (6-7, 6-3, 6-3) — went to the No. 1s, who arrive in Rotterdam with 16,974 points each against 14,017 and 13,889 for Bea and Paula. There is a record in between: **Triay is the first player in Premier Padel history to reach 200 tour wins**, achieved in Madrid.

## Sánchez-Ustero and Calvo-Fernández's lightning rise

**Ariana Sánchez and Andrea Ustero** are the third seeds and arrive off the biggest women's result of the year: the **Paris Major**, won 6-2, 6-0 in the Roland Garros final. It is their third title of 2026 (after Riyadh, where they already beat Triay-Brea 3-6, 6-1, 6-4, and Valencia), and Sánchez is world No. 3 with 14,029 points. Her racket in the catalog: the <ProductRef id="adidas-arrow-hit-light-2026" />. And on the other side of the net, the fastest-rising pair in the sport's history: **Claudia Fernández and Martina Calvo**, together since July 7, with three finals in four tournaments, two wins over the No. 1s and Calvo's precocity record (18 years old, the youngest champion of a major tournament, in London). Fernández is world No. 6 (11,817 points) and already qualified for Barcelona: her <ProductRef id="bullpadel-wonder-2027" /> is in our catalog.
