**Chingalán is staying put.** Alejandro Galán confirmed on Sunday in an interview with MARCA — published hours before the World Cup final, which he watched from home with a splint immobilising his right hand — that he will keep playing alongside Fede Chingotto in 2027. And he added the detail everyone was waiting for: they will do it with a new team, after Jorge Martínez announced he is stepping away from the professional tour's benches.

The news lands in the most awkward stretch of his season and, at the same time, the calmest moment for his future. Galán has missed the **Kuwait Major**, the **Doha World Cup** — Javi Leal took his place in the Spain squad — and the **Dubai P1**, which starts today without him or Chingotto. The cause is a right-hand injury that still has no return date and that, for now, is being treated without surgery.

## "It's a new team and Chingalán stays together in 2027"

The question had been hanging in the air for weeks. Talk of a split resurfaced after the European swing, with early exits in Rotterdam and Düsseldorf and a level below the one they showed in the first half of the season. Galán shut the speculation down: **"It's a new team and 'Chingalan' stays together in 2027 — there are your two headlines."**

The "new team" is not a figure of speech. The Spaniard explained they are building their own structure for next season and that the line-up will be announced in due course: **"We are preparing a new team to go for it in 2027."** What is already public is the departure of Jorge Martínez, his coach since his teens, from travelling on tour: **"He gave me the opportunity at 15 and I have spent half my life sharing a team with him,"** said Galán, who also remembered Mariano Amat, the man beside him every day for a decade.

His faith in his partner is explicit. **"I believe in what we are capable of doing, in the level we have shown together and in what we can still improve,"** he said, before defending Chingotto against the criticism: "What Fede has improved in these three years is outrageous."

## The injury: a blow from his own racket in Milan

The injury did not come from a fall or a bad movement by an opponent. It happened in the **round of 16 at the Milano P1**, on a recovery in mid-air. Because Galán holds the racket very low, the end of the handle struck the knuckle of the fourth finger of his right hand. **"The racket destabilised the knuckle, I felt the pain and it tore,"** he recalls.

The diagnosis is **damage to the ligament tissue that holds the tendon** of that finger. With little blood supply, it does not heal like a muscle, and the swelling built up over the following days until he could no longer compete. **"We opted for conservative treatment,"** he explains; the hand stays immobilised and scans will show how it progresses. Surgery has not been ruled out: **"The doctors treating me told me it has only happened to Matteo Berrettini, and he went under the knife."**

On timelines, there is no certainty: "There are no deadlines and no clear evidence in this sport that gives me an estimated time." What lies ahead are the **Mexico Major** (November 23-29, Acapulco) and the **Barcelona Finals** (December 10-13), and he cannot guarantee he will make either: entries close well in advance and he is still waiting on MRI results.

## Paris, the match that came down to one ball

If there is a turning point in Galán's year, it is the **Paris Major** final. He and Chingotto led **5-2 in the third set**, one ball away from the title, and Tapia and Coello came back. The blow was more mental than mathematical: winning would have put them ahead in the Race and given Chingotto his first Major.

"It was very hard emotionally afterwards," he admits. "You could see so many positive things…" Doubt crept into the following events, above all Rotterdam and Düsseldorf, where the pair lost the determination that defines them. Even so, Galán reads the season differently: **"In terms of level, I think it has been the year we have played best throughout the whole season,"** he says, while accepting the missing piece: **"The Majors shape the season and this year we have not managed to win one."**

## The dream: world No. 1 on both sides

At 30, and about to become a father for the first time — "a little girl on the way", he revealed — Galán allows himself a goal nobody has achieved on tour: **becoming world No. 1 while also playing on the right**. "I would like to be the first player to be No. 1 on both sides," he says. It is not the immediate plan, but it is the horizon.

In that hypothetical scenario, his partner would be "the best". Asked whether that means Tapia, he answers: **"Right now, of course."** All of it remains a conversation, though: his priority is still to reclaim the throne with Chingotto.

## What comes next: Acapulco and Barcelona

The end of the calendar will not wait. The **Dubai P1** is being played this week without the world No. 2 pair, and then come the **Mexico Major** and the **Barcelona Finals**, the last two events of the year and two of the biggest points on offer. Galán wants to be there: "I hope I can make it." But there will be no date until the hand responds.

In the meantime, the tour is without its defensive reference. Tapia and Coello already have the year-end No. 1 wrapped up, Triay and Brea are fighting for the women's crown, and the season closes with one open question: when Galán comes back, and in what form.
