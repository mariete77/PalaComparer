The Bologna Final 8 has its first semi-final, and it came at a price. **Italy**, the three-time defending champions and hosts, needed the doubles to get past **South Korea (2-1)** in the tightest tie of the week, and on Friday they meet **Czechia**, who had swept Canada on Tuesday without dropping a rubber. The other two quarter-finals are decided today: **Germany v Great Britain (10:00)** and **Spain v Austria (not before 17:00)**, with Carlos Alcaraz's fitness still up in the air.

## From military service to beating Italy's top man

The script looked written. **Lorenzo Musetti** opened the tie with a **6-0, 7-6(5)** win over Seongchan Hong in 90 minutes, and the afternoon looked like a formality against the Korean side, the surprise guests of this Final 8. Then came **Soonwoo Kwon**, the world No. 139 who lost the entire 2025 season to mandatory military service in his country.

Kwon beat **Flavio Cobolli** **3-6, 7-6(3), 7-6(5)**. Cobolli, Italy's highest-ranked player this week with Jannik Sinner absent, arrived straight from the ATP Finals in Turin, fought back from 5-3 down in the second set and saved a match point in the third, but both tie-breaks went the Korean's way. "It's impossible to explain," Cobolli admitted afterwards.

## Four set points and one hour 54 minutes

At 1-1, the tie came down to a doubles rubber that had everything. **Matteo Berrettini and Andrea Vavassori** beat **Hyeon Chung and Kwon** **7-6(2), 7-6(11)** in one hour 54 minutes, after saving **four set points** in the second set and closing out a tie-break that ran to 13-11.

The win sends Italy into a **fifth consecutive semi-final** and extends their run to **15 straight Davis Cup ties won**: their last defeat came against Canada in the 2023 group stage. Captain Filippo Volandri had left doubles specialist Simone Bolelli out to bring Berrettini in for this rubber, a call that paid off.

## Czechia reach a semi-final 12 years on

Tuesday's tie was far cleaner. **Tomas Machac** (No. 59) opened with a **6-2, 3-6, 6-2** win over Liam Draxl (No. 154), and **Jiri Lehecka** (No. 14) sealed it **7-6(4), 6-2** against **Felix Auger-Aliassime** (No. 17).

That second match tells the story: Auger-Aliassime created **nine break points** in the first set and converted none; Lehecka saved every one and won 42 per cent of his return points against one of the tour's biggest servers. The Canadian had won the previous three meetings and led the head-to-head 3-1; Bologna makes it 3-2. He leaves without a reward from a team missing both Shapovalov and Diallo to injury.

It is Czechia's **first semi-final since 2014**. The captain is **Tomas Berdych**, who won the trophy as a player in 2012 and 2013 and now has his team two wins from the title. His decision to pick Machac ahead of a higher-ranked Jakub Mensik worked out.

## Friday: two contrasting runs

Friday's semi-final (16:00) pits a team winning on momentum against one that has just ended a 12-year wait. Italy are chasing a **fourth straight title**; Czechia their first trophy since 2013. The one caveat: Italy have turned the doubles into their safety net — yesterday being the latest example — and the workload on Berrettini and Vavassori, who also play singles and arrive from the hard-court swing, is the only visible crack in Volandri's line-up.

## Today: Germany v Great Britain and Spain's opener

Thursday hands out the last two tickets. **Germany**, with Alexander Zverev as their No. 1, open at 10:00 against **Great Britain**; **Spain** follow, not before 17:00, against **Austria**.

**David Ferrer's** squad is **Alcaraz, Jodar, Merida, Pedro Martinez and Granollers**. Daniel Merida came in for the injured Alejandro Davidovich, and Alcaraz is the big unknown: he won the ATP Finals on Sunday, arrived in Bologna on Monday, did not hit on court on Tuesday and is carrying discomfort in his left thigh and right knee. Holding him back for the doubles with Granollers and trusting Jodar and Merida in the singles is on the table.

Austria, captained by Jurgen Melzer, bring **Rodionov, Neumayer, Schwaerzler, Oberleitner and Miedler**, plus an awkward stat for Spain: the Austrians lead the head-to-head **3-2**. The winner faces the Germany-Great Britain winner on Saturday at 12:00.
