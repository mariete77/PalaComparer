With the Dubai P1 done, the tour crosses the Atlantic for **the last Major of 2026**. The **GNP Mexico Major Premier Padel** runs from **23 to 29 November** at the **Arena GNP Seguros in Acapulco**, the third edition of the only Major on the American continent — and it arrives with three questions open: the women's No. 1, the return of the two big absentees, and the last tickets to the Barcelona Finals.

## Acapulco, where padel was born

This is no ordinary venue. Mexico's Tourism Ministry and the organisers both recall that it was in Acapulco that **Enrique Corcuera built the world's first padel court in 1969**. Half a century later the Pacific resort hosts one of the four biggest events on the calendar, with a **12,000-seat** arena and global-event numbers: **more than 35,000 spectators** expected across the week, an economic impact above **250 million pesos** and broadcast in **281 territories**, with a potential audience north of **100 million viewers**.

It is also a reminder of how forced the 2026 calendar has been: the suspension of the Qatar Major pushed Kuwait into October, so Acapulco closes a 25-event season that began in Riyadh in February and ends in Barcelona in December.

## The world No. 1s are back; Galán is a maybe

**Agustín Tapia and Arturo Coello** skipped the Dubai P1 on "medical advice" from their team, without specifying the problem, and they did so with the job done: by winning the Kuwait Major — **their 13th title of the season** — they had already secured the **year-end world No. 1 for a fourth straight season**. They arrive in Acapulco rested, with a chance to add to a tally that is already historic.

The other side of the coin is **Alejandro Galán**. The injury to the ring finger of his right hand has already cost him the Kuwait Major, the Doha World Cup — which Spain won without him — and the Dubai P1. His return in Acapulco is not confirmed and depends on how the hand responds in the final training sessions; the player himself said last week that **Chingalán continues in 2027**, but with a new team.

## The women's No. 1 gets signed in Acapulco

The women's season has been the most unpredictable in memory, yet the ending has a clear script. **Gemma Triay and Delfi Brea**, No. 1 in both the ranking and the Race, lost in the Dubai quarter-finals to Jensen and Icardo but still control their own fate: **reaching the semi-finals in Acapulco** is enough to seal No. 1 for a second consecutive year.

Behind them the map has shifted. **Paula Josemaría and Bea González** were mathematically eliminated from the race after losing the Dubai final, and **Ari Sánchez and Andrea Ustero** no longer have a route either. The pair arriving in the best form is **Claudia Fernández and Martina Calvo**: three titles in nine events, the last one in Dubai a week after winning the World Cup with Spain.

## The Barcelona maths

The Major awards **2,000 points** to the winning pair, the last big pot of the season before the **Premier Padel Finals, 10-13 December at the Palau Sant Jordi**, where the **top 16 of the FIP Race** qualify.

That is where Dubai changed things. **Gonzalo Alfonso** moved into that top 16 after reaching his first Premier Padel final; **Tino Libaak finished 70 points short** and needed the title to get in. At a Major one extra round is worth hundreds of points, so the Acapulco draw is effectively a play-off for the last places: whoever sits between 12th and 20th in the Race is playing their season across three matches.
