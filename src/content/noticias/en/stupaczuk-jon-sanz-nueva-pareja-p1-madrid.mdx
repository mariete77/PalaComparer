The most anticipated move of the summer is now official: **Franco Stupaczuk and Jon Sanz** will play together from the **Madrid P1**, which takes place at the **Movistar Arena** in the first week of September. The news, first reported on August 10 and confirmed days later with a joint social media presentation, brings one of the biggest storylines of the Premier Padel partner market to a close.

## The partner shuffle shaking up the top of the rankings

It all started on August 6, when Stupaczuk announced on social media the split from **Mike Yanguas** after they were eliminated in the round of 32 at the London P1. The pair, who had joined forces in January, posted a disappointing first half of the season by their own standards: **six semifinals, just one final**, and losing their spot as the No. 3 pair in the rankings to **Juan Lebrón and Leo Augsburger**. "Our paths separate once again and we put an end to our padel together. I wish you the best," wrote the Argentine.

The breakup triggered a domino effect. Stupaczuk has chosen left-handed Navarrese player **Jon Sanz** as his new partner, who until now shared a project with **Coki Nieto**. And the two "left behind" players of the move, Nieto and Yanguas, have decided to play together again: they already formed a solid duo throughout 2025, reaching numerous semifinals and giving the world's best pairs a scare on several occasions, though without making a single final.

## Stupa returns to playing with a lefty

The new pair has a clear tactical reading: Stupaczuk once again bets on a left-handed profile by his side, a variation he had not used since his stage with **Pablo Lima** in 2022. The right-handed player from Chaco will play on the drive side, while Jon Sanz will take the backhand side, his natural wing as a lefty. On court, expect a highly electric duo, with two hitters capable of speeding up the rhythm of any point.

When it comes to gear, Stupa's return to a left-handed profile also changes the aerial game: Sanz's backhand with rebound off the wall will be key to breaking defensive blocks. The side you play on conditions your paddle choice more than you might think: a drive player looking for ball speed is not the same as a backhand player who lives off the bandeja and the víbora.

## Jon Sanz: "I felt stuck in a loop"

In an interview with MARCA, Jon Sanz explained the reasons behind the change with complete honesty: "I felt stuck in a loop, even with myself, and I told myself I had to turn it around, do something different." The player from Pamplona was self-critical and made it clear the decision was purely sporting: "It's not about Coki's level, nor Maxi [Grabiel]'s, nor my coach's."

The Navarrese player also denied that the rankings played any part in his choice: "The points were not part of my decision," he said, revealing that he only found out a few days earlier that they would start as fifth seeds in Madrid. His goal for the second half of the year goes beyond titles: "What I think I'm going to have are new hopes. I'm excited about a new project, a new team. Right now I'm motivated and eager."

Worth remembering: Jon Sanz already knows what it takes to win titles. In his first stage alongside Coki Nieto they won the **Bordeaux P2** and the **Barcelona Premier Finals**, while in their second spell together they never reached a final.

## A video-game presentation

The new duo introduced themselves with a video produced by **Twenty4 Studios** in which, using artificial intelligence, both players are recreated as video-game characters. Published on both players' social media, the clip has become one of the most viral of the week and sets the tone for the project.

## Coki and Yanguas, fourth seeds in Madrid

Coki Nieto and Mike Yanguas' reunion will be immediate: they will debut at the same tournament, the Madrid P1, as **fourth seeds**, edging out Stupaczuk and Jon Sanz by a very small margin in points, who will start as **fifth seeds**. The gap is so small that their performances in the coming events will decide who keeps that coveted fourth spot, which Paquito Navarro also aspires to.

## A context of absolute dominance

The move lands in a season defined by the hegemony of **Arturo Coello and Agustín Tapia**, who lead the rankings with more than 6,000 points of advantage over **Alejandro Galán and Federico Chingotto**, second. After Stupa-Yanguas lost the No. 3 spot, **Lebrón and Augsburger** complete the podium.

In the individual standings, Stupaczuk (7th) and Yanguas (8th) remain in the upper tier, with Paquito Navarro (9th) and Coki Nieto (10th) close behind. With the **Barcelona Finals** (only the top 16 in each category) as the big end-of-season event, every tournament in this second half will be decisive in securing a ticket to the Palau Sant Jordi.

Professional padel proves, once again, that rankings are as volatile as the pairings themselves. The second half of 2026 promises plenty of excitement.
