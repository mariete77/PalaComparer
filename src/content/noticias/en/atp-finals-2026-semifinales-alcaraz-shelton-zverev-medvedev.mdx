Friday closed the round robin and the ATP Finals have their four names: **Carlos Alcaraz, Ben Shelton, Alexander Zverev and Daniil Medvedev**. Alcaraz took care of business against **Flavio Cobolli in 65 minutes (6-4, 6-1)** and finished Ivan Lendl Group **3-0 without dropping a set**; Medvedev claimed the last spot by beating **Frances Tiafoe 6-3, 6-4** in the winner-takes-all night match. On Saturday, two semi-finals at the Inalpi Arena: **Alcaraz-Shelton not before 2:30 p.m.** and **Zverev-Medvedev not before 8:30 p.m.** (CET).

## The two groups came out identical

The draw has turned out symmetrical down to the detail:

Both group winners arrive unbeaten and without losing a single set. Both runners-up arrive with one defeat and two wins, an identical 4-3 in sets. And there is one more coincidence that should worry the favourites: **both runners-up have already beaten Saturday's opponent** at least once, and in Shelton's case it came in the biggest match the two have ever played.

## Alcaraz-Shelton: a 4-1 that hides a 3:33 a.m. finish

The head-to-head reads **4-1 for Alcaraz**, but the Spaniard's only defeat was his most painful of the year. In the **2026 US Open quarter-finals**, Shelton beat him **6-7(5), 6-1, 6-3, 1-6, 7-6(10-7)** in four hours 28 minutes, coming back from 3-5 down in the deciding tie-break and closing it with a 146 mph serve. The match ended at **3:33 a.m.** in New York, the latest finish in tournament history, and it was the American's first win over a top-3 player. Alcaraz was the defending champion.

The most recent meeting, though, is from this month and on the surface Turin uses: **the Paris Masters final, 7-6(5), 7-5 for Alcaraz** indoors on hard court. That is the stat that matters most for Saturday, because the Inalpi Arena plays exactly like that, and because the Spaniard arrives with **40 hard-court wins in 2026**, behind only Medvedev's 42.

Alcaraz is chasing his **first Finals title**: this is his fourth appearance and last year he lost the final to Jannik Sinner. Shelton, 24, is **the youngest American in the semi-finals of this event since Andy Roddick in 2004**.

The physical question remains. On Thursday he had an MRI on his left hamstring and right knee and cancelled his practice session; on Friday he played without any sign of discomfort and put it simply: *"The way I felt on court today was incredible."* If the leg holds up over three sets of full intensity, the match comes down to the return: Shelton's serve is the hardest to read in the field, and on a fast indoor court one break per set is usually enough.

## Zverev-Medvedev: the only head-to-head Zverev does not win

The year-end No. 1 has beaten almost everything put in front of him this week: **3-0, six sets won and none lost**, after saving five match points against Fils. On Monday he collected the trophy that confirms him as **2026's year-end No. 1**, the first German to finish a season on top.

But there is one column he cannot win: **Medvedev leads 14-8** across 22 meetings. He is the only semi-finalist with a winning record against the German, and he arrives on his best form of the week after winning the match that was worth a semi-final spot: *"I love to win,"* he said after the 6-3, 6-4 over Tiafoe. For the Russian it is his **fourth Finals semi-final and his first since 2023**, and he has **42 hard-court wins**, the highest tally of the season.

There is a very concrete way to read this matchup. Zverev serves, dictates with the forehand and wants the point short; Medvedev returns from deep, with flat, long trajectories, and turns every point into a race. On a court that rewards the serve, the Russian needs rallies to go past five shots; the German needs them to end in three.

**Alcaraz and Shelton share a generation and a head size, not a philosophy**: the Pure Aero 98 carries a denser 16x20 and a 66 RA, the stiffest frame of the four, built to hit with spin without giving up control; the EZONE 98 drops to 63 RA and opens the pattern to 16x19 so Shelton's flat serve and forehand come out with less effort.

**Zverev and Medvedev are exact mirrors**: nearly identical strung weight (323 against 320 grams) and the same head size, but the German plays a **16x19 at 61 RA** — flexible, for flat, deep hitting — and the Russian an **18x19**, the densest pattern in the field, to control the low bounce and return with a taut trajectory. If the match goes long, that density favours Medvedev.
