The China Open never got its final — but it crowned a champion: **Novak Djokovic** lifted his **seventh title** in Beijing yesterday, his first since 2015, after **Alex de Minaur retired with an adductor injury**. The Australian won the opening game of the second set and could not go on: 7-6(3), 0-1. The 39-year-old Serb claims the **102nd title** of his career, his first of 2026, and keeps a perfect record intact: **34 matches, 34 wins in Beijing**.

## A final without a final: 67 minutes of war and two physios

The match began as a serve-by-serve chess game: **a 67-minute first set without a single break**, both men holding firm. In the tie-break the Serb's class told, 7-6(3). But the effort took its toll on both.

After the opening set, both players needed medical attention. Djokovic was feeling his **abdomen** — the price of his week's battles with Zverev and Medvedev — while De Minaur went to the locker room to have his **left thigh** strapped. He returned with a white wrap, won the first game of the second set... and that was it: unable to compete at full tilt, he retired.

"I don't feel great," De Minaur told the crowd. "I want to say sorry to the amazing fans that came to watch... I didn't want to finish like this." In press he was more precise: "I was feeling quite good, then I felt something midway end of the first set. It gradually got a little worse. I felt something in the adductor. I don't know what it is yet. I've got to get some more scans." And he made his plan clear: "My plan is to go to Shanghai and get scans."

The world No. 9 leaves with the bittersweet taste of a big week: "Everything except for the end was really positive. There was a lot of good tennis. I played some great stuff. I felt amazing on the court."

The last three men's matches never went the distance: **Medvedev was defaulted** in the semi-finals for hitting a spectator with a ball, **Hurkacz retired injured** in the other semi (6-4, 3-2, with De Minaur in control) — [the full semi-final day here](/noticias/china-open-2026-final-djokovic-de-minaur) — and **De Minaur did the same in the final**. An unusual close to a tournament that had delivered a great week of tennis.
</Callout>

## 102 titles, an eternal 34-0 and a 39-year-old record

- **34-0 in Beijing**: seven titles and zero defeats. Nobody has ever beaten him there.
- **Title No. 102**: one shy of Roger Federer's 103 and seven short of Jimmy Connors' Open Era record (109).
- **His first title of 2026** and a **21st consecutive season** with at least one trophy.
- **At 39**, he is the first ATP champion aged 39 or over since Ken Rosewall (Hong Kong, 1977).
- On the way he beat **Bu Yunchaokete** in a demanding three-setter and top seed **Alexander Zverev** in the quarter-finals.

On what still drives him two decades in, Djokovic pointed to "the grit and the dedication and the kind of never-die spirit that really is always with me". And in the trophy ceremony he did not hide what it meant: "I always want to visualise myself with the trophy, particularly here in Beijing. But I was also quite aware that reality is a little bit different these days." It has been a year, he admitted, of "physical and health issues": "I was just trying to find the good feeling on the court."

## Shanghai starts today: no Sinner, plenty of doubts

No rest for the tour: the **Rolex Shanghai Masters** opens its main draw today (October 7-18) — without the world No. 1. **Jannik Sinner announced yesterday that his season is over**: he needs treatment for the knee injury he has carried since Wimbledon.

<Callout title="Sinner: 'The 2026 season is officially over'">
"Me, together with the doctors, have decided that I need a course of treatment on my knee, which means that the 2026 season is officially over," the Italian wrote on social media. "But at the moment, I also need to think about my long-term career. I will do everything possible to get back to 100 per cent." His absence — he has not played since winning Wimbledon in July — leaves the race for the year-end No. 1 wide open.
</Callout>

**Djokovic is seeded 10th**, with a bye into the second round and a tough draw: Hurkacz or Duckworth first, 24th seed Musetti projected in round three, and Zverev, Shelton and Tiafoe in his half. Whether he plays remains to be seen: during Beijing he said he needed to "see how I feel" before deciding, and he left the final with abdominal trouble. **De Minaur stays in the draw**, a doubt until scans arrive. And at the top: **Valentin Vacherot defends** the unlikeliest title of 2025 (that final against his cousin Arthur Rinderknech) while **Carlos Alcaraz**, fresh from the Japan Open title, leads the bottom half.
