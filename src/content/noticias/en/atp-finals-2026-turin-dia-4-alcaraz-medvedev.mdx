Turin finally has its first semi-finalist and the tournament, at last, a clear hierarchy: **Alexander Zverev is in charge and he has not dropped a set**. The World No. 1 dismissed Ben Shelton **6-2, 7-6(4)** in one hour and 34 minutes, becoming the first player to qualify for the last four at the 2026 Nitto ATP Finals. Arthur Fils, meanwhile, claimed his first win at the event and left Rafael Jodar out of the tournament through injury.

Today, **Wednesday 18**, day 4 brings two matches with the same label: win and you lead. **Alcaraz-Medvedev** (not before 14:00 local) decides top spot in the Ivan Lendl Group, and **Cobolli-Tiafoe** (not before 20:30) sends the first man home.

## Tuesday: Zverev, without a crack

The German gave nothing away: break in the first game, a second break in the fifth and **4-1 inside 20 minutes**. Shelton, who had won his debut and needed this match to reach the semi-finals, hung on with his serve —he saved **two break points at 4-4 (15/40)** with a volley and an ace— but ran out of room in the tie-break.

The numbers explain it better than any adjective: **24 unforced errors from Shelton to Zverev's 10**, and a **7-0** head-to-head that no longer needs interpretation. The previous chapter was the **2026 US Open final**, also won by the German. It is also his **first Top-5 win** since beating Shelton himself in Turin last year.

<Callout title="The stat of day 3">
Zverev is into the semi-finals for the fifth time in his Finals career. And he is there with **two sets won out of two** and without conceding more than four games in any set. His group, the John McEnroe Group, has an owner: him.
</Callout>

## Fils ends a 15-year wait

The other story of Tuesday is French. **Arthur Fils came from 4-6 down to beat Rafael Jodar 6-2, 6-2**, the first win by a Frenchman at the Finals since **Jo-Wilfried Tsonga in 2011**. He is also the **first player born in 2004 or later** to win a match at the season-ending championships.

And it has to be said precisely: **the match turned when Jodar's body said enough**. The Spaniard, who turned 20 this year and is the youngest debutant in the field, took the opening set with brave tennis, but in the second he began to pile up unforced errors and could only win four games across the remaining two sets. The physical problems —a medical timeout, medication and a consultation with the tournament doctors— left him empty and, at **0-2**, mathematically out.

For Fils it is a lifeline: he is still alive, and on Thursday he plays Zverev for a place in the last four.

Thursday brings **Fils-Zverev** and **Shelton-Jodar**, and here is the interesting part: if Fils beats the already-qualified Zverev and Shelton takes care of Jodar, **there will be a three-way tie at 2-1** decided by the percentage of sets and games won among the tied players. In other words, Zverev —with nothing left to play for— becomes the group's judge whether he likes it or not.

## Today: a fast court and Medvedev's best bullet

The first heavyweight clash is **Alcaraz-Medvedev**, a rivalry that stands at **7-3 for the Spaniard** and that has been split this year: Medvedev won their **Indian Wells semi-final (6-3, 7-6(3))** and Alcaraz won their **Vienna semi-final (6-4, 7-6(2))**. Their only previous meeting at the Finals came in 2023, also for Alcaraz (6-4, 6-4).

But the context matters more than the history. The court at the Inalpi Arena is fast and indoor, and Medvedev knows it: this is where the serve hands out free points and the flat ball finds lines.

> "I think there are two realities. One: Alcaraz is an amazing player and he can beat anyone anywhere. And two: I think this court is the best chance to beat him, because you can get maybe some free points on the serve, maybe put some winners because it's fast, which is not easy to do against him, because he's probably the best mover we have ever seen, together with Novak."

Alcaraz sees it the same way, only framed as punishment: "It's like going to war, because you have to win every point. Sometimes you have to win the point two or even three times, with very long rallies."

The detail that says the most about this match: **both racquets declare the same swingweight (324)** but get there by different routes. Medvedev's **18x19** is the only dense pattern in the TFight range and it exists for a reason: flat trajectory, control and less variation off the bounce. Alcaraz's **16x20** is Babolat's compromise to fit the spin of the Aero family into a player's frame without losing the sweet spot.

On a fast, low-bouncing court, the dense pattern usually wins the argument against heavy topspin. That is exactly what Medvedev needs to drag the match onto his terms: long rallies, little height, few flourishes.

## Cobolli-Tiafoe: lose and go home

The night session is an elimination match with no nuance. **Cobolli and Tiafoe are both 0-1**, and whoever loses says goodbye to the semi-finals.

Cobolli, the only Italian in the singles field, arrives with the label of **2026 Roland Garros finalist** —he lost to Zverev in five sets, 6-1, 4-6, 6-4, 6-7(5), 6-1, after 4h16— and with the home crowd, which in his debut pushed him all the way to a first-set tie-break against Medvedev. Tiafoe, a US Open semi-finalist this year, is the oldest debutant in the field at 28 and needs to rediscover the serve that carried his season.

## Doubles: the champions are upset

In the Todd Woodbridge Group, **Marcelo Arevalo and Mate Pavic came from behind against defending champions** Heliovaara and Patten (6-7(5), 6-3, 10-4) and are into the semi-finals. Arribage and Olivetti stay alive after beating Luz and Matos (6-4, 7-6(4)), who are eliminated. On Thursday, **Heliovaara-Patten against Arribage-Olivetti** decides the other spot. Today: Harrison-Skupski vs Bolelli-Vavassori (11:30) and Granollers-Zeballos vs Krawietz-Puetz (not before 18:00).
