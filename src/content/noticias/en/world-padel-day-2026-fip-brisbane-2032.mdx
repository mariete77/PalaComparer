20 October 2026 will go down as the day padel got its official celebration: the International Padel Federation (FIP) and Premier Padel have formally recognised the date as **World Padel Day**, formalising a day the global padel community had already embraced. The announcement, made from Lausanne, comes with the campaign through which the sport renews its Olympic ambition: **joining the programme of the Brisbane 2032 Games**.

## The numbers FIP is putting on the table

The World Padel Report 2026, the study underpinning the bid, paints a sport in full worldwide expansion:

- An estimated **40 million amateur players** across **191 countries and territories** on all five continents.
- **Women account for 47%** of participants — a gender balance padel can showcase against many Olympic sports.
- More than **91,000 courts** worldwide, the foundation the player base keeps growing on.
- The CUPRA FIP Tour visits **59 countries**, the FIP Promises junior circuit reaches 36, and Premier Padel is broadcast in **244 territories**.
- The economic impact study commissioned by FIP (conducted by OpenEconomics) estimates padel will contribute **€8.6 billion to global GDP in 2026** and support around **236,000 full-time equivalent jobs**.

"Padel can bring Brisbane 2032 a global community, exciting competition for women and men, and a sport that people can discover and continue playing long after the Games," said **Luigi Carraro**, President of the International Padel Federation. The campaign closes with a line that sums up the ambition: *"Padel is fit for the future. Padel is ready for Brisbane 2032."*

## The nod to Australia: padel at the Sydney Opera House

The bid is not just paperwork. On 15–16 August, the **Padel Invitational** presented by UBS installed a temporary court on the **Sydney Opera House**'s Northern Broadwalk, with the harbour as a backdrop. Delivered by Padel Australia together with Premier Padel and FIP, the event brought together international padel stars, local club players and Australian tennis great Pat Rafter. A full-scale demonstration — in the country that will host the Games — that padel's format works on any stage.

One important nuance: **the day is official, the Olympic spot is not**. Which sports join Brisbane 2032 is decided by the IOC on the organisers' proposal, and nothing has been announced. Padel has already featured at the European Games, and its presence at the South American, Mediterranean and Asian Games strengthens the file — but inclusion remains an ambition, not a fact.

## What comes next

The calendar doesn't stop: the **Kuwait Major** (26–31 October) is the circuit's next big event, the only Major of the season in the Middle East after its upgrade from P1 to Major. And the Olympic machinery keeps turning: FIP will keep leaning on its 115 national federations and its presence at continental Games to press its case for the IOC's decision on Brisbane 2032.

In the meantime, padel now has what the big sports have: a day of its own on the calendar.
