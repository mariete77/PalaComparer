On the spec sheet of any mid-to-premium paddle you'll read "Carbon 12K" or
"Carbon 18K". Almost everyone assumes a higher number means a better paddle.
That's not what it measures.

## What the K means

The K indicates how many **thousands of filaments** each thread of the carbon
weave carries. 3K is 3,000 filaments per thread, 18K is 18,000.

More filaments per thread means a tighter weave with smaller squares. That
changes two things:

<SpecList>
  <SpecRow label="Fewer K (3K)" value="More open weave, more elastic, softer feel" />
  <SpecRow label="More K (18K)" value="Denser, stiffer weave, drier feel" />
</SpecList>

## What you'll notice on court

**A 3K gives more.** The ball stays an instant longer on the paddle face and
that gives you a sense of control and an easy exit.
<ProductRef id="nox-ml10-ventus-control-3k-2026" /> goes that way.

**An 18K gives back sooner.** The face deforms less, so the response is more
immediate and drier. It hits harder if you have the technique to leverage it,
and transmits more vibration if you don't.

**A 12K sits in the middle**, which is exactly why it's the most common.
<ProductRef id="nox-at10-genius-12k-xtrem-2026" /> and
<ProductRef id="nox-at10-genius-18k-2026" /> are the same model with a
different weave: a good place to see the isolated effect.

<Callout title="Beware the easy comparison">
Two paddles with the same 18K can feel completely different if one runs EVA
Soft and the other EVA Hard. The core weighs more on the final feel than the
face weave. Always look at both together.
</Callout>

## So is more K better?

No. It's **different**. An 18K in the hands of someone who still doesn't hit
center is an uncomfortable, hard paddle. A 3K in the hands of a smasher is a
paddle that comes up short.

The right order to decide is: first the shape, then the core, and the carbon
weave last, to fine-tune the feel.
