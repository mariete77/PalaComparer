The **CUPRA Germany Premier Padel P2** (Castello Düsseldorf, €264,534 prize money) wrapped up its week on Sunday with blockbuster finals: **Arturo Coello and Agustín Tapia** beat Franco Stupaczuk and Jon Sanz **6-2 7-6(5)** for their **11th title of the season and third in a row** — after the Paris Major and Rotterdam P2 — while **Martina Calvo and Claudia Fernández** lifted the women's trophy by seeing off Ari Sánchez and Andrea Ustero **6-3 6-0 in just 1h08**, completing a full revenge for the last Major's final. Each champion pair takes home **600 points** in the FIP Ranking and the Race to the Barcelona Finals.

## Coello-Tapia: European treble and a 70-7 season

The men's final was the exact rematch of Rotterdam's, and the script barely changed: the world No. 1s now own **58 titles as a pair** — eleven of them this season — and stretch their 2026 record to a devastating **70-7**. The match itself, though, was tighter than a week ago. Stupa and Jon came out firing, breaking the champions' serve to lead **2-0**, but Tapia-Coello answered with a run of six straight games to pocket the first set 6-2. In the second, the challengers broke again and **served for the decider at 5-6**, only for the Golden Boys to raise their level at the key moment and take the tie-break **7-5** after 1h24.

For Stupaczuk and Sanz, a third consecutive final defeat (after the Lyon FIP Platinum and Rotterdam) leaves a bittersweet taste: their push towards the top of the ladder keeps rolling, as they posted a **second straight win over Galán-Chingotto** (7-6(3) 6-4 in the semi-finals), a result that locks in their status as Tour No. 4 seeds from the Milan P1 onwards. In the other semi, Tapia-Coello gave Juan Lebrón and Leo Augsburger no chance (6-2 6-3 in 1h05).

## Calvo-Fernández pull off the Paris Major revenge

The women's final was a statement. **Claudia Fernández and Martina Calvo**, who on Saturday had knocked out Paula Josemaría and Bea González in a **3h07** thriller (6-4 5-7 7-6(5), saving **two match points**), controlled the match from start to finish against the reigning Paris Major and Rotterdam champions. Sánchez-Ustero earned **eight break points** in the first set and converted none: frustration crept into their game and they conceded a bagel in the second set with no answers to Fernández's attacking play and Calvo's lobs.

It is the **complete revenge** of the Roland Garros final, where Sánchez and Ustero had won with the same authority (6-2 6-0). For the Spanish duo — Calvo, 18; Fernández, 20 — it is their **second title together** since pairing up in July, after the historic London P1, and it confirms that the youngest pair ever to win a Premier Padel event is no longer a promise: they are a top-table reality. Sánchez-Ustero, meanwhile, played their third consecutive final (Paris and Rotterdam, both won; Düsseldorf, lost) and keep chasing Triay-Brea, whom they had eliminated in the semi-finals for the third time this season (7-5 3-6 7-5).

## A week of records at the Castello

Beyond the finals, Düsseldorf produced another milestone: Friday's quarter-final day was **the fastest in Premier Padel history**, with eight matches, eight two-set wins and just 131 games. The week also saw Coki Nieto and Mike Yanguas withdraw due to the Navarrese's physical issues, their spot going to a lucky loser in the men's draw.

## What's next: Milan, Kuwait and the closing stretch

The tour does not rest: this very week the **Milan P1** kicks off (October 10-18), the last European stop of the year before the final swing: **Kuwait Major** (October 23-31), **Dubai P1** (November 8-15), **Mexico Major** (November 23-29) and the **Premier Padel Finals in Barcelona** in December. In between, from November 2 to 7, the **FIP World Cup in Doha** puts the team title on the line, with Stupa and Jon Sanz on the Argentina and Spain lists respectively. Coello-Tapia head into that stretch with a cushion, but a third straight title only raises the bar of what is already a historic season.
