The expensive mistake with a first paddle is buying the one a pro uses. Those
paddles are built for people who hit center 90% of the time. If you're not there
yet, it'll frustrate you and load up your elbow.

These are the four numbers you should actually look at.

## 1. Weight: less is more at first

Aim for the low end of the range, between **355 and 365 g**. A light paddle
comes up faster on the volley and reaches awkward balls better. The extra power
of a heavy paddle is worthless if you're late to the shot.

## 2. Balance: low

Low balance = the weight sits close to your hand = the paddle handles itself.
It's the same thing you want from the weight, which is why the two almost always
go together on beginner paddles.

## 3. Core hardness: soft

A soft rubber absorbs vibration and gives the ball back more easily. That's what
protects your elbow while your technique settles. Hard rubbers only make sense
once you're generating the arm speed yourself.

## 4. Shape: round

Large, central sweet spot. It forgives off-center hits, which at the start are
most of them.

<Callout title="Realistic budget">
Between €60 and €120 you have perfectly good beginner paddles. Above that you're
paying for materials you won't exploit yet. Save the money for the second
paddle, once you know what you like.
</Callout>
