Six matches, six wins, one set dropped. Spain closed their second day at the **Doha World Padel Championships** with another pair of 3-0s —**Paraguay** in the men's draw, **Great Britain** in the women's— and have their quarter-final place sealed in both events. The difference is that Juanjo Gutiérrez's men are already mathematically top of Group B, while the defending champions are not: the women's group is decided this morning against **Brazil** (12:00 CET, Grand Stand 1).

## Five games in twelve sets

**Carolina Navarro's** team have played two ties and have conceded **five games across twelve sets**. Two against Germany on Monday, three against Great Britain on Tuesday. It is not a hot streak; it is depth, because Navarro has rotated her bench like someone dealing cards.

**Andrea Ustero and Martina Calvo** opened against the British pair with a **double 6-0** over Norton and Jackson. **Marta Ortega and Bea González** matched the tone with **6-1, 6-1** against Gibson and Nicholas. And **Paula Josemaría and Ari Sánchez**, rested on Monday, closed it out **6-0, 6-1** over Phillips and Tordoff. All eight players in the squad have now played inside two days; Monday's line-up was Fernández, González, Josemaría, Calvo, Triay, Ortega, Ustero and Sánchez in different combinations. Few nations can afford that, and in a five-day format with a knockout every day it is a real advantage.

The only issue, if there is one, is the schedule: the level goes up now. Great Britain and Germany were the soft part of the group; Brazil are something else.

## Leal is no longer the substitute

In the men's draw, the story of the day had a name: **Javi Leal**. He came into the squad when **Ale Galán** was ruled out injured and on Tuesday he played his first World Cup match as a starter, alongside **Mike Yanguas**, to open the tie against Paraguay. They won **6-4, 6-3** against González and Dehnike, with Leal reading the game well and Yanguas closing at the net. The point that had to open a tie, opened without drama.

Then came the steamroller. **Paquito Navarro and Juan Lebrón** went through Petit and Popovich **6-0, 6-2** in the most one-sided match of the day. **Aimar Goñi and Arturo Coello** finished it **6-1, 6-3** against Miranda and Rodríguez. One note for the coaching staff: **Coki Nieto is the only one of the eight who has not played yet**. Gutiérrez has used seven players in two ties and won six matches out of six. Top spot in the group is already his on the numbers: with 12 sets won and one lost, no other team can pass Spain even if they lose today.

## Germany blow Group A open

The result that changed the map came in the women's draw. **Germany came from behind to beat Brazil 2-1**: the Brazilians took the first match **6-1, 6-0** and then fell apart. Germany won the next two **6-2, 6-2** and **6-1, 6-1** — that is **24 games to 6** across the last two matches. A collapse that brings the group back to life and turns today's Spain v Brazil into more than a formality.

The maths are simple. Spain lead with **12 sets won and none lost**, six matches out of six; Brazil arrive with 4-2 in matches and 8-5 in sets. A Spanish win gives them top spot. Only a **0-3** defeat would leave Navarro's team second and send them into the other half of the draw, potentially facing Argentina in the semi-finals. That is the scenario nobody wants.

## Today: top spot and four tickets

The third day closes the group stage with all eight matches in each draw at the same time. At **12:00 CET** the women play: **Spain v Brazil** on Grand Stand 1 —a court carried by **Red Bull TV**—, **Germany v Great Britain** on court 7 and **Argentina v France** on Grand Stand 2, with Argentina defending their 2-0.

In the afternoon, from **16:00**, the men: **Spain v UAE** on Grand Stand 2, **Sweden v Paraguay** on the centre court —the tie that decides the second ticket in Group B—, **Argentina v France** on court 5, **Italy v Brazil** on court 6 and **Portugal v Chile** on court 7. In Group D, Italy and Brazil are both 2-0 and play each other for top spot; in Group C, Portugal defend first place against Chile with Germany waiting behind them.

## Argentina are already showing their rackets

While Spain manage their workload, **Argentina** have not dropped a set in two ties: **6-0 in matches and 12-0 in sets**. And on Tuesday **Agustín Tapia** finally appeared: he made his tournament debut alongside **Tino Libaak** —a partnership that does not exist on tour— and they won **6-4, 6-2** against Richters and Roper. **Stupaczuk and Arce** won 6-1, 6-3; **Augsburger and Chingotto**, 6-3, 6-4. Argentina have eight starters and no weak spot.
