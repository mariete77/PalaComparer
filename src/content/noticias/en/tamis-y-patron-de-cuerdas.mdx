A racket spec sheet carries seven numbers. Three of them tell you almost
everything. Let's go in order of real importance.

## Head size

The surface area of the face, in square inches. It drives everything else.

## String pattern

Written as "16x19": 16 vertical strings by 19 horizontal.

- **Open (16x19)** — the squares are bigger, the string bites the ball and it
  climbs. More spin, more launch, shorter string life.
- **Closed (18x20)** — tighter mesh. Flatter ball and more control, but you have
  to generate the spin yourself.

around topspin.

## Swingweight: the forgotten number

The weight you **feel when moving** the racket, not on the scale. Two 300 g
rackets can feel completely different if one has swingweight 310 and the other
330.

Below 310 it's quick to handle and forgives being late. Above 325 it hits harder
but demands you prepare the swing earlier.

<Callout title="The classic mistake">
Buying by weight in grams and ignoring swingweight. Static weight tells you what
it weighs in the hand; swingweight tells you what it'll weigh on you in the
third set.
</Callout>

## The three that remain

- **Stiffness (RA)** — how much the frame bends. Higher = more power and more
  vibration to the arm. Above 70 with a delicate elbow, be careful.
- **Balance** — millimeters from the butt. Higher = more head = more power and
  less maneuverability.
- **Length** — 68.58 cm is standard. A few millimeters more give reach on the
  serve at the cost of control.

## How to use this

Fix the head size first by your level, then the pattern by whether you're a
spinner or a flat hitter, and use swingweight to fine-tune. The rest are
nuances.
