Today, **Sunday, October 4**, the **CUPRA Rotterdam Premier Padel P2** (Rotterdam Ahoy, €264,534 in prize money) wraps up its week with finals day: two matches on Centre Court from **16:00 Spanish time** (11:00 in Argentina), both on **Red Bull TV** and **Movistar Plus in Spain**. And both finals come with their own history: **Ari Sánchez played her 100th professional final on Saturday** — the youngest player ever to reach that mark — and **Stupaczuk-Sanz will play their first Premier Padel final as a pair**. At stake in each draw: the title and up to **300 FIP points**.

## Ari Sánchez: the youngest player ever to reach 100 finals

Saturday's women's semi-finals produced two straight-set results and one big exit: **the No. 1 seeds, Gemma Triay and Delfi Brea, are out of Rotterdam**, falling at the penultimate hurdle for the second straight tournament (in Paris, Calvo-Fernández had been their executioners). This time it was **Ari Sánchez and Andrea Ustero**, superior from start to finish, winning **6-2 6-3** in one hour and 19 minutes.

The win carries double value for Ari: it snaps a **three-match losing streak against the No. 1s** (Bordeaux P2, London P1 and Madrid P1) and delivers a historic milestone. At 29, the Catalan becomes **the youngest padel player ever to reach 100 professional finals**, a club that, among active players, only Gemma Triay and Alejandra Salazar had joined before.

> "I'm very happy to reach my 100th final, but I don't think about it. I feel so lucky to play padel… I'm super happy to play one more final," Magic Ari said after the win.

## Josemaría-González, chasing a seventh title of the season

In the other semi-final, **Paula Josemaría and Bea González** got past the No. 4 seeds, **Claudia Fernández and Martina Calvo, 6-3 6-4**. The young London P1 champions had played the final in three of the four tournaments since they formed as a pair; the only time they fell before the final was, precisely, against Paula and Bea.

Perlamita reach their **seventh final of the season** with two goals: to win a title for the first time since **June, at the Valladolid P2**, and to become the pair with the most trophies of 2026, trimming the No. 1s' lead at the top of the FIP Race.

## Tapia-Coello, one step away from stretching their Race lead

In the men's draw, **Agustín Tapia and Arturo Coello** got the job done the way they usually do at this tournament: dropping a set, as in the quarter-finals, and then closing it out. The No. 1s beat **Momo González and Lucas Campagnolo 6-1 3-6 6-1** and are the **only survivors of the top four seeds** in a draw turned upside down.

Defending champions at the Ahoy and fresh from retaining their Paris Major title, the Golden Boys have an extra prize in play today: with Galán-Chingotto out since the quarter-finals, lifting the trophy would give them **1,020 more points of lead over their closest rivals in the FIP Race**.

## Stupa-Sanz: a first final together after winning the battle for the top 4

Their opponents are one of the pairs of the week: **Franco Stupaczuk and Jon Sanz**, who posted a **6-0 6-4 in 79 minutes** against Mike Yanguas and Coki Nieto — a replay of the FIP Gold Belgrade final from a few weeks ago, which also fell the Hispano-Argentine way — after **knocking out Galán-Chingotto in Friday's quarter-finals**.

Saturday gave them more than a final: with the win over their former partners they **secured the No. 4 seed for the two remaining tournaments of the European swing** (Germany P2 and Milan P1). It will also be their **first Premier Padel final as a pair**, a week after losing the FIP Platinum Lyon final to Leal-Guerrero.

> "We are building a great team. We're back in another final after the FIP Platinum in Lyon last week… we like playing on Sundays! I hope this win is the start of something big," Stupa summed up.
