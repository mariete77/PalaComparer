One thing about the **Dubai P1** was settled since Tuesday: with Tapia-Coello and Galán-Chingotto out, the tournament was going to crown new champions. What was not written was **who**. After two men's semi-finals settled in **one hour and four minutes** and **one hour and two minutes** — the quickest of the week — Sunday's final will be **Libaak-Alfonso against Lebrón-Augsburger**, and the women's final **Calvo-Fernández against Josemaría-Bea González**.

Neither final has ever been played before. This is the third Dubai P1 in history and the first one that does not repeat finalists.

## Lebrón and Augsburger: know-how, and a final without the world No. 1s

**6-4 6-2 against Abbate and Dal Bianco in 1h04.** That is how the second seeds dispatched the story of the tournament, the pair who came through qualifying and had knocked out Momo González and Campagnolo in the quarter-finals. **Flavio Abbate becomes the first Italian player to reach a P1 semi-final**, and he did it with a partner, Franco Dal Bianco, who was also in that round for the first time and with whom he has played barely two events. They leave with the best week of their careers and the feeling that 4-4 in the first set was their last moment in the match.

For **Juan Lebrón**, Sunday's final is the **71st of his career**, which takes him past Juani Mieres into sole ninth place on the all-time list of final appearances. **Leo Augsburger** plays his ninth as a professional and his second of the season in Premier Padel. As a pair it is their **fifth final of the year** — Cancún, Brussels, Málaga, Milan and now Dubai — with **one title**, Brussels, precisely the only one of the five without a Coello-Tapia pair on the other side.

And that is the detail that rewrites the season's script: for the first time in 2026, Lebrón and Augsburger play a final **without the world No. 1s in front of them**.

## Libaak and Alfonso: a first final, at 21

There was no contest in the other semi-final: **6-1 6-4 against De Pascual and Garrido in 1h02**. The Argentinians went after the match from the first game and left the pair who had knocked out Yanguas-Nieto and Stupaczuk-Sanz with no room to react. De Pascual and Garrido still close the best tournament of their year: **Garrido's first semi-final in a year and Juani De Pascual's first in Premier Padel**.

**Tino Libaak is 21 and plays his first final on the tour**. **Gonzalo Alfonso, 30, plays his third**: the previous two came in 2025 alongside Tolito Aguirre, in Gijón and Cancún, and he lost both. They have been together since the summer, growing event by event — quarter-finals in Rotterdam, Düsseldorf and Milan, semi-finals in Kuwait — and in Dubai they made the leap.

The result pays twice. With the 600 points secured by reaching the final, **Alfonso moves into the top 16 of the 2026 Race** that grants a place at the Barcelona Finals, and **Libaak is 70 points short**. If they win on Sunday, he is in too.

## The head-to-head nobody expected in a final

Libaak and Alfonso have met Lebrón and Augsburger twice in 2026. Both times in the round of 16. And they won both:

- **Madrid P1, September: 7-6 7-5.**
- **Kuwait Major, October: 4-6 7-5 6-1.**

The third is a final, and the stage is different: Silingo's pair have played five finals this year and know how to win a P1; the Argentinians arrive knowing they have beaten them twice and with nothing to lose. On paper, Lebrón and Augsburger are favourites. On court, the 2-0 head-to-head is the one piece of evidence that cannot be argued with.

## Women's draw: two first-time finals and the No. 1 waiting in Mexico

Order prevailed in the women's draw. **Josemaría and Bea González (2) beat Ari Sánchez and Ustero (3) 6-4 7-5** in 1h38 and **secure the world No. 2 spot** for the season. The other semi-final was the quickest of the day: **Claudia Fernández and Martina Calvo (4) swept past Jensen and Icardo (6) 6-3 6-1 in 1h16**, with the pair who had knocked out Triay-Brea paying for the week's workload.

With Ari-Ustero out, **Triay and Brea remain the virtual No. 1s of the year**: if they reach the quarter-finals of the Mexico Major (23-29 November) they will mathematically seal the world No. 1 for the second year running. And with the top pair at home, the Dubai final is a double opportunity for Josemaría and Bea: a title and confirmation that they are the pair who have grown most in the closing stretch.
