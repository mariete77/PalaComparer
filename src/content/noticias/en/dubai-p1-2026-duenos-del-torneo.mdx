The **Dubai P1** runs from **November 8 to 15**, pays **€479,068** and hands the champions **1,000 points**. It is the last P1 of the season — after it come only the Mexico Major (November 23-29) and the Barcelona Finals (December 10-13) — but above all it is the tournament with the shortest history on the calendar. Two editions, and on the men's side a single name on the trophy.

## The tournament Coello and Tapia have never lost

2024 and 2025. **Arturo Coello and Agustín Tapia** are the only pair to have lifted the trophy in Dubai. The second one took the most work: they arrived on two straight defeats against **Galán and Chingotto**, who were riding a three-title run, and beat them in the final **6-3 6-4** without conceding a single break point in the second set. That was their **eleventh title of 2025**.

In 2026 they land with a different momentum: **five consecutive titles** — Paris, Rotterdam, Düsseldorf, Milan and Kuwait —, **thirteen across the season** and the year-end No. 1 already sealed, their fourth in a row. Win on Sunday the 15th and Dubai becomes their **sixth straight title** and their third in a row here. They debut on **Wednesday the 11th** in the round of 32, like every other seed, all of them exempt from the first two rounds.

## Bea González: two Dubais, two partners

The women's story is stranger still. **Bea González** has won both editions, but not with the same partner: in **2024** she did it with **Delfi Brea**, in **2025** with **Claudia Fernández**. Last year's final was also against Brea and **Gemma Triay**, the world No. 1s, beaten **6-1 7-5** with 18 winners and just six unforced errors in the first set. That afternoon left Claudia and Bea with **five wins in eight meetings** against the top pair.

This season Bea plays with **Paula Josemaría** and is the second seed. She arrives with **six titles in 2026**, including a run of **five consecutive trophies** — the longest women's streak of the season — but **she has not won since June 28**, when she took the Valladolid P2. Since then she has let slip finals that were within reach: Málaga, where they served for the title; Paris, with five match points; Rotterdam and Milan. In Dubai she can do something nobody has done: win the same P1 three years in a row with three different partners.

Her first opponents in the round of 16 will be either **Collombon-Sharifova** or **Fassio-Virseda**.

## Brea, the other half of the story

**Delfi Brea** has been in both Dubai finals: she won the 2024 one with Bea and lost the 2025 one alongside Triay against Bea and Claudia. Now she returns as **world No. 1** with the latest big title in her bag: the **Kuwait Major**, coming back from **3-6 6-1 6-1** against Ari Sánchez and Ustero. It was the pair's **seventh title of the season** and **Triay's 59th**, the most decorated player in the history of women's padel.

Their lead in the FIP Race is **2,780 points** over Sánchez-Ustero, with **4,500 still on the table** across the three tournaments left. In plain terms: Dubai cannot mathematically seal No. 1, but it can all but close the door.

Their first match comes out of qualifying: **Saiz-Talavan** or two qualifiers. In the quarter-finals, the projected opponent is **Icardo-Jensen**.

## The women's draw at a glance

The top four pairs are exempt from the first two rounds and go straight into the round of 16. The projected quarter-finals:

- **Brea-Triay (1)** against **Icardo-Jensen (6)**
- **Salazar-Osoro (8)** against **Fernández-Calvo (4)**
- **Sánchez-Ustero (3)** against **Ortega-Araújo (5)**
- **Guinart-Alonso (7)** against **Josemaría-González (2)**

**Ari Sánchez and Andrea Ustero** are the form pair: they won three of the last four tournaments — Paris, Rotterdam and Milan — and arrive fresh from winning the Doha World Cup with Spain. And in the fourth line, **Claudia Fernández** is the draw's other Dubai champion: the one who won in 2025 beside Bea, now with **Martina Calvo**.

## The numbers

The prize money climbs every year: **€470,000** in 2024, **€474,500** in 2025 and **€479,068** this time. The champion takes **1,000 points**, half of a Major. **Red Bull TV** broadcasts from the quarter-finals to the final; earlier rounds are on Premier Padel's YouTube channel.
