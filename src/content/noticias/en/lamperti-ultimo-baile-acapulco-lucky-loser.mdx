On Monday night, **Miguel Lamperti** lost 7-6, 7-6 to Aris Patiniotis and Thijs Roper. Almost two hours to fall out of the **Mexico Major** main draw — the tournament he had already marked as his last Premier Padel event. He had just turned 48, on 11 November, and the farewell was closing on him in Acapulco qualifying.

It didn't close.

## A withdrawal hands him the spot back

A few hours after that defeat, a withdrawal from the main draw left one place open. It went to him, alongside **Octavio Álvarez**. Tuesday's official order of play already lists them as **"[LL] Lamperti / Álvarez"**, second match on Cancha Mifel, against **Enrique Goenaga and Guillermo Collado**.

Mundo Deportivo caught the reaction: *"I got a lucky loser spot for once. I'm so happy — I get to play again,"* he said, jumping, when he learned he was back in.

## What a lucky loser actually is

At every event, qualifying hands out the last places in the main draw. Pairs that lose in the **final qualifying round** don't leave straight away: they stay on standby in case someone withdraws before the first ball. The FIP even sets a sign-in deadline — 3:30 pm local on Tuesday — because the spot goes in order and on paper.

It is a second chance that almost never comes. This time it came, which is why a 48-year-old with half a lifetime on tour ended up jumping around the locker room.

<Callout title="The last dance, in numbers">
Lamperti (Bahía Blanca, 11-11-1978) sits at **No. 118 in the FIP ranking** with 498 points on the 19 October list. After Acapulco only the **Barcelona Finals (7-13 December)** remain, reserved for the year's best pairs: for him, the international circuit ends here. He will keep playing FIP events and other tournaments.
</Callout>

## The opponent is no gift either

Goenaga (24, Palma, right side) and Collado (21, Madrid, left side) are not wildcards: Collado arrives at No. 39 in the ranking with 1,600 points. On paper they start as favourites, as Padel Magazine noted in its preview of the day. On court, favouritism in a Tuesday first round is worth what it's worth.

There is a prize behind it, too: whoever wins that match meets **Tino Libaak and Gonzalo Alfonso** in the round of 32, the No. 9 seeds and Dubai P1 finalists.

Lamperti and Álvarez have been playing together since September. At the Kuwait Major they reached the round of 16 — beating Javi Garrido and Juani de Pascual, then losing to Coello and Tapia — so the level needed to compete in a 48-pair draw is already proven.

## 48 years old and a round racket

Part of this story is told by the racket in his hand. Lamperti is the **ML10** player, Nox's line that carries his name and arrives in 2026 as the **ML10 Ventus Control 3K**: round shape, balanced weight, wide sweet spot, 3K carbon faces, HR3 Color EVA core and 360-375 grams. ## Thirty years on tour

His career has passages that explain his popularity better than any ranking. He started playing at 12, trained against the wall and reached Spain with Alejandro Lasaigues. With Matías Díaz he was world No. 3 behind Belasteguín and Juan Martín Díaz. In **2016**, at the World Championship in Portugal, he sealed the decisive point for Argentina against Álvaro Cepero and Juan Lebrón (6-4, 6-4), with Cristian Gutiérrez as his partner. And in **2021** he agreed to play with a 19-year-old almost nobody knew: Arturo Coello.

## Acapulco already said goodbye once

Last year, at this same event, Lamperti also came through qualifying — with Juani de Pascual — and won his first main-draw match against Perino and Piotto, 6-3, 6-4. That time the tournament gave him one more match; this time it has given him another. At 48, ranked 118th, every main draw at a Major is extra time nobody gets to sign twice.
