The **US Open 2026** women's final is played **today, Saturday, September 12**, at Arthur Ashe Stadium, and it needs no introduction: **Aryna Sabalenka vs Elena Rybakina**, the reigning No. 1 against the No. 1 of Monday. Two rackets, two opposite philosophies of the game — and both models are in our catalog.

## Sabalenka and the three-peat nobody has signed since Serena

The Belarusian top seed is chasing what only two women have achieved in tournament history: **three consecutive US Open titles**. The last to do it was **Serena Williams (2012-2014)**. To get there she dismantled Jessica Pegula **7-5, 6-2** in the semifinals with a serving masterclass: 29 winners to the American's 12, 7 aces and 14 of 19 second-serve points won (sources: USOpen.org, Yahoo Sports). It is her fourth straight final at Flushing Meadows and her eighth straight hard-court Grand Slam final.

Her weapon is the **Wilson Blade 98** — <ProductRef id="wilson-blade-98-v10-2026" /> in its tenth generation: 98 sq in, 323 g strung and the **FORTYFIVE** layup that doubles flex without losing stability. The classic formula for the aggressive baseliner who wants everything to come back. Wilson has also released a limited "US Open Edition" Blade 98 v10 inspired by Sabalenka (98 sq in, 305 g), the special paint job lighting up the Ashe these weeks (source: Wilson).

## Rybakina and the VCORE 100 of the new No. 1

On the other side of the net, **Elena Rybakina** plays her **first US Open final** after coming back from a set down against Coco Gauff, 3-6, 6-4, 6-4, silencing the New York crowd. And she does it with no ranking pressure: **the world No. 1 will be hers on Monday**, ending Sabalenka's 99-week reign — the thirtieth leader since 1975 (sources: USOpen.org, Sky Sports).

Her racket is the **Yonex VCORE 100, 8th generation** — <ProductRef id="yonex-vcore-100-2026" />: 100 sq in, 300 g strung, a 16x19 pattern and a build focused on **aerodynamic spin** (ISOMETRIC, Aero Trench, 2G-NAMD Flex Force) with which she hits flat and opens the ball at will. The same model used on tour by Paolini and Machac, strung with POLYTOUR FIRE (source: Yonex).

## The technical keys of the Blade–VCORE duel

- **Weight and balance**: the Blade 98 v10 lists 323 g strung with a 330 mm balance; the VCORE 100, 300 g and 320 mm. The Wilson is heavier and loads mass toward the hoop — more plow-through on every shot; the Yonex is more maneuverable and quicker to prepare.
- **Head size**: 98 vs 100 sq in. The VCORE makes up for the extra head with an equally open pattern (16x19), which gives it more sweet-spot room and access to spin.
- **Stiffness**: 62 RA for the Blade vs 65 RA for the VCORE. The Wilson is more elastic and comfortable; the Yonex returns more energy with less effort.
- **Style**: the Blade is the aggressive-control racket — for the player who trusts their timing and swings full without the ball sailing. The VCORE is the modern-spin racket — for the player who builds with heavy, high-bouncing balls before finishing.

The head-to-head favors Sabalenka **10-7 across 17 meetings**, but the last final between them went to Rybakina: this year's Australian Open, **6-4, 4-6, 6-4** (source: USOpen.org). That stat breaks one way or the other today.

## Meanwhile, in the men's draw

While the women's final is being played, the other men's semifinal, **Shelton–Tiafoe**, is being decided this very night in New York (Tiafoe has taken the first set), and its winner will play for the title on Sunday against **Alexander Zverev**, who beat Karen Khachanov 6-3, 7-6(7), 7-6(6). The German thus reaches his second Grand Slam final of the year after winning Roland Garros (sources: USOpen.org, The Washington Post).
