The main draw of the **GNP Mexico Major** opens today, Monday November 23, at the **Arena GNP Seguros in Acapulco**. After the weekend's qualifying rounds, the tournament moves into its big week (23-29) as what it is: **the last Major of 2026**, worth **2,000 FIP points** to the champions and a **€1,044,849** prize pool split across both draws.

The men's throne is not at stake —**Tapia and Coello** sealed the year-end No. 1 in Kuwait a month ago— but Acapulco does decide two things that matter: **who finishes the year as women's No. 1** and **who takes the last places at the Premier Padel Finals**, December 10-13 at the Palau Sant Jordi. And it arrives with the cut line squeezed to the millimetre by what happened last week in Aguascalientes.

## Aguascalientes left the cut at 50 points

The season's last **FIP Platinum** ran from November 15 to 21 and handed the title's 300 points out in Saturday's two finals. In the men's draw, seventh seeds **Lucas Bergamini and David Gala** beat **Edu Alonso and Aimar Goñi** **6-4, 6-2**, having already taken out three higher-seeded pairs on the way. In the women's, top seeds **Aranzazu Osoro and Claudia Jensen** lifted the trophy by coming back against **Jessica Castelló and Laia Rodríguez**: **1-6, 7-5, 7-5**.

Those 300 points redraw the map. **Osoro** is back in the Barcelona fight after a season in which her best Premier Padel result had been a quarter-final. And **Edu Alonso**, the runner-up, added 180 points that do not put him in the top 16: from **3,090** he moves to 3,270, below the **3,300** that marked the cut before the Mexican event.

## The last men's place: five points

The standings published by Premier Padel on November 20, before the Aguascalientes final, leave the hot zone like this:

Above them, **Jon Sanz (4,290), Paquito Navarro (4,184) and Martín Di Nenno (3,980)** are practically booked: they sit more than 700 points clear of 17th. The drama is right on the line: **Momo González defends 15th by five points** over Gonza Alfonso, and his own partner, **Campagnolo**, cannot overtake him because their results will be identical for the rest of the season.

The best-placed outsider is **Coki Nieto**. The Madrid native has played only **21 ranking tournaments** —he missed the Germany P2 and the Milano P1 through injury— and the Race counts a player's **22 best results**, so almost everything he earns in Acapulco goes straight into his tally. **Libaak**, world runner-up with Argentina, is 70 points off the cut and also arrives in form.

<Callout title="The Finals stay open until the 29th">
All 16 places in each draw will be mathematically confirmed on Sunday 29, when the Major ends. The Barcelona event runs December 10-13 at the Palau Sant Jordi and pays more per player than any other tournament on the tour.
</Callout>

## The women's No. 1, one final away

**Gemma Triay and Delfi Brea** arrive with **14,200 points**, **2,600 clear** of **Ari Sánchez and Andrea Ustero** (11,600), and with the Kuwait Major title in the bag. Per the calculations published by Premier Padel, **reaching the final in Acapulco would give them the No. 1 for a second straight year**; some specialist media lower the requirement to the semi-finals depending on how the dropped results fall. Either way the cushion is huge: **Bea González and Paula Josemaría** (10,940) can no longer catch them.

## Salazar, the only one with no safety net

In the women's draw the most fragile place has a name. **Alejandra Salazar** is 15th and was **the only one of the players involved who did not play Aguascalientes**, so her rivals were free to close the gap while she watched. Behind her: **Verónica Virseda** (16th, 2,810), **Nuria Rodríguez** (2,760), **Giulia Dal Pozzo** (2,730) and now Osoro, with the 300 points from her title.

The context adds weight to every match: Salazar **retires at the end of this season**. One last Finals at the Palau Sant Jordi, among the best, would be a farewell on court. To get there she needs Acapulco to go well.

## When it is played and what time to watch

The main draw runs **Monday 23 to Sunday 29**. The men's first round opens on Monday; the women come in on Tuesday. Up to the quarter-finals, matches stream on the **Premier Padel YouTube channel**; from the quarters on, **Red Bull TV** depending on your country. The finals are on **Sunday 29**.

Mind the clock: **Acapulco is seven hours behind Spain**. A 5pm local final on Sunday is midnight in Madrid, and the evening sessions land in the small hours. If you are staying up, at least know what you are staying up for.
