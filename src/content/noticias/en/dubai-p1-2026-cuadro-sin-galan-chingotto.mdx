Two days after Spain lifted the World Cup in Doha — [both finals, point by point](/noticias/mundial-padel-doha-2026-final-espana-campeona) — the tour hands every player their regular partner back. The **Dubai P1** runs **November 8-15** at the **Hamdan Sports Complex** and is the **last P1 of the season**: only the Mexico Major and the Barcelona Finals come after it. The draw was made on Sunday and it opens with the absence that has defined the end of the year: **the world No. 2 pair is not here**.

## No Galán, no Chingotto — again

**Ale Galán** has still not recovered from the physical problems that have kept him out of the last few tournaments — he also missed the World Cup, where Javi Leal came in for him — and **Fede Chingotto** is left without a tournament because of his partner's injury. It is not the first time this autumn: the pair also missed the **Kuwait Major** for the same reason, and the tour has had to learn to play without its defensive reference.

The withdrawal also lands when the race for No. 1 is already settled. **Agustín Tapia and Arturo Coello** secured the 2026 year-end top spot during the Asian Major, so Dubai does not decide the men's throne: it decides who arrives in the best shape for Mexico and Barcelona.

## Tapia and Coello, job already done

The world No. 1s arrive in Dubai on **five consecutive titles** — Paris, Rotterdam, Düsseldorf, Milan and Kuwait — and with the ranking wrapped up. In Doha they split up: **Coello played the World Cup with Coki Nieto**, and **Tapia began the event with Libaak and finished the final alongside Chingotto**. Now they are back together with the usual plan: Tapia dictates from the drive, Coello closes on the backhand.

## The men's draw: 16 seeds wait until Wednesday

The format squeezes the schedule. The **sixteen seeded pairs are exempt from the round of 64** and do not debut until **Wednesday the 11th**, in the round of 32. Until then, the draw belongs to qualifiers and unseeded pairs.

The seeding order is: **Tapia-Coello (1)**, **Lebrón-Augsburger (2)**, **Stupaczuk-Jon Sanz (3)** and **Paquito Navarro-Di Nenno (4)**. In the top seeds' half sit **Mike Yanguas and Coki Nieto**, **Fran Guerrero and Javi Leal**, **Libaak and Gonza Alfonso**, **Bautista-Bergamini**, **De Pascual-Garrido** and **Maxi Sánchez-Sanyo**. In the other half, Lebrón-Augsburger's, wait **Momo González-Campagnolo**, **Tello-Arce**, **Edu Alonso-Aimar Goñi**, **Jofre-Esbrí**, **Álex Ruiz-Javi García** and **Barahona-Piotto**.

## The women's draw: Triay-Brea close on the throne

The women's draw carries a different kind of tension, because the No. 1 spot **is still on the line**. **Delfi Brea and Gemma Triay** arrive as top seeds and are close to sealing the year-end top ranking. They also arrive with the latest title in the bag: they won the **Kuwait Major coming from behind**, and in Dubai they defend the status of the pair to beat.

Behind them, the rest of the top four is not standing still: **Bea González and Paula Josemaría (2)**, **Ari Sánchez and Andrea Ustero (3)** — three of the last four tournaments, with Paris, Rotterdam and Milan — and **Claudia Fernández and Martina Calvo (4)**. All four pairs are exempt from the round of 32 and **debut on Thursday the 12th in the round of 16**.

## Where and when to watch

The tournament runs **November 8-15** at the Hamdan Sports Complex in Dubai, with the men's seeds entering on Wednesday the 11th and the four women's seeds on Thursday the 12th. **Red Bull TV** broadcasts from the quarter-finals to the final; earlier matches are available on Premier Padel's YouTube channel. After Dubai, the tour closes with the **Mexico Major (November 23-29)** and the **Barcelona Finals (December 10-13)**.
