The European swing ends here: today, **Sunday 18 October**, the **Oysho Milano Premier Padel P1** closes the season's penultimate P1 at the Allianz Cloud with a double bill from **15:00 CEST (10:00 in Argentina)**: the women's final first, the men's right after. At stake: **€479,068** in prize money and **1,000 FIP points** per draw for the champions. And both finals come with their own storylines: a **rematch of the Brussels final** in the men's draw and a battle for the women's throne with three pairs still mathematically in the race for year-end No. 1.

## The 0-6 that woke the Wolf

The semi-final between **Galán-Chingotto** and **Lebrón-Augsburger** was two matches in one. The first, an exhibition by the world No. 2s: **6-0 in 29 minutes** with **74% of points won**, 85% behind their first serve and a perfect 100% behind the second. The second, the most serious comeback of the tournament: **0-6 6-2 6-2 in an hour and 24 minutes**, with Lebrón and Augsburger taking 72% of the points in the second set and 67% in the third.

> «At that moment we stopped doubting ourselves and started playing. I think that's when our best padel comes out», Lebrón said after the match.

The man from El Puerto de Santa María returns to a Milan final three years later: he won the **2022 and 2023 editions alongside Galán**, and the Allianz Cloud remains the court where he has lifted the most trophies. For **Leo Augsburger** it is the first P1 final of his career; together they have only won one title, April's **Brussels P2** — and they won it precisely against the pair waiting for them today.

## Tapia-Coello: fourth final of the swing and Coello's 300

In the other semi-final, **Agustín Tapia and Arturo Coello** once again had the measure of **Stupaczuk-Sanz** (**6-4 6-3** in 1h18), their third meeting in three weeks after the Rotterdam and Düsseldorf finals. The win leaves two record numbers behind: the world No. 1s are on a **17-match winning streak**, and Coello has become **the first player to reach 300 wins on the Premier Padel tour**.

> «Individual awards do not interest us», the Valladolid native told the press, focused on the team.

Champions in Milan in **2024**, the Golden Boys today chase a **fourth consecutive title** on the European swing — Paris, Rotterdam, Düsseldorf and Milan—, their **twelfth of 2026** and another step towards closing the year as No. 1, where they lead Galán-Chingotto by 2,200 points in the Race. Across the net stands the pair that came back on them in the Brussels final (2-6 6-3 6-3): the only one that already knows what it takes to beat them on a Sunday with a trophy on the line.

## Ari-Ustero vs Josemaría-Bea: the Rotterdam final, again

The women's draw repeats the line-up from the Ahoy two weeks ago. **Ari Sánchez and Andrea Ustero** swept past the world No. 1s, **Triay-Brea**, **6-1 6-4 in 1h15**: the third time in a row they beat them on this swing and their **fourth consecutive final** — Paris, Rotterdam, Düsseldorf and Milan—, with two titles already bagged (the Paris Major and Rotterdam).

**Paula Josemaría and Bea González** booked their spot the hard way: **6-2 7-6 over Fernández-Calvo**, the exact rematch of the Düsseldorf final the young pair won a week ago. And with an image for the ages: the match point lasted **more than two minutes**, all four players defending and lobbing, until Paula closed it out with a decisive smash.

Ari and Ustero won the Rotterdam final (**6-4 6-4**); in Brussels, back in April, Perlamita clinched their third straight title there. Today there is also a throne at stake: with the final and four tournaments to go, **Triay-Brea, Ari-Ustero and Josemaría-Bea all remain in mathematical contention for year-end No. 1**. Whoever lifts the diamond ring takes a giant step.
