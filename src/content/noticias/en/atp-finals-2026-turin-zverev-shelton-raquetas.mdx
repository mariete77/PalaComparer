Turin heads into its third day of competition with both groups still open and with one certainty nobody can take away: **Alexander Zverev will finish 2026 as World No. 1**. The German received the ATP Year-End No. 1 trophy on court at the Inalpi Arena on Monday night, on the same day **Carlos Alcaraz and Daniil Medvedev** opened their accounts in the Ivan Lendl Group with two uncontested wins.

Today, Tuesday 17, day 3 brings the most attractive match of the opening week: **Zverev against Ben Shelton** not before **20:30** local time, after the **Fils-Jodar** scheduled for 14:00.

## Monday: Alcaraz and Medvedev, no alarms

**Alcaraz (2) beat Frances Tiafoe (7) 6-2, 6-2 in 69 minutes.** The Spaniard arrived in Turin on the back of consecutive titles in Paris and Vienna, and **20 wins in his last 22 matches** since returning at the US Open after four months out with a wrist injury. He raced to 4-0 and, when the match demanded nerve, saved **three break points at 3-2** in the second set. Tiafoe, debuting at 28 — the oldest man to debut at the event since Anderson and Isner in 2018 — drops to 0-1 in a career-best season with 44 wins.

**Medvedev (4) beat Flavio Cobolli (5) 7-6(4), 6-3.** The 2020 champion returned to the Finals after missing 2025 and found himself up against the only Italian in the singles field, backed by a loud home crowd. The first set went to the tie-break with **six break points saved by Cobolli**; in the second, a single break in the seventh game was enough. Medvedev won **83% of his first-serve points** and put it plainly: "It's a very fast indoor court, so the serve is important." The Russian has four titles and 53 wins in 2026, second only to Zverev's 66.

In doubles, **Krawietz-Puetz (7) swept past Harrison-Skupski (2) 6-1, 6-3**, while **Granollers-Zeballos (3) beat Bolelli-Vavassori 6-4, 6-4**. The year-end No. 1 pair, **Heliovaara-Patten**, also received their trophy in Turin.

## Zverev: two Slams and the trophy he was missing

The German's season is the best of his career and the numbers leave no room for argument: **Roland Garros and the US Open**, semi-finals in Australia, his first Wimbledon final and **66 wins**, the most on tour. At 29 he became the **30th player in history to reach No. 1** and the **first German to finish a year on top**. He is also the oldest first-time year-end No. 1: 29 years and 217 days, ahead of Agassi's 29 and 214 in 1999 and Murray's 29 and 197 in 2016.

He is the fourth active player with a year finished at No. 1, after Djokovic (eight times), Alcaraz (two) and Sinner (one). And he arrived with a warning for anyone expecting the trophy to soften him.

<Callout title="The No. 1 speaks">
"To be the Year-End No. 1 is the most special thing. It's the biggest achievement of my career. Winning two Grand Slams, making the final of Wimbledon… I'm having the best year of my life and now, holding this trophy, is the perfect end," Zverev said during the ceremony.
</Callout>

## Today: Zverev-Shelton, and a 6-0 that doesn't tell the whole story

The head-to-head says **6-0 to Zverev**, and the latest chapter was **this year's US Open final, in four sets**. They also met at the 2025 Finals, where the German won in straight sets. Shelton, however, arrives with his first win at the event already in his pocket: **6-2, 3-6, 6-3 against Arthur Fils** in Sunday's night session.

The Zverev who showed up on Sunday was the best version: **84% of first serves in, ten aces and only eight second serves in the entire match** to see off Rafael Jodar in **1h27** (6-4, 6-4), with one break per set. Repeat that first-serve percentage and the American's task becomes very steep; he needs to serve at least as well as he did on Sunday for his return — his best weapon — to come into play.

## Fils-Jodar: the debutants' duel

The other John McEnroe Group match (14:00) pits two players who are both 0-1 against each other, and they know each other well. **Jodar leads 2-1** in their head-to-head: Fils won in Barcelona earlier this year and the Spaniard took the next two, **Washington and Montreal, both by the same score: 7-6(5), 6-3**.

Jodar, who turned 20 this season, is the **first man to qualify for the Finals in the same season as his tour-level debut**, and he arrives after a year in which he went from prospect to reality. Fils, for his part, knows what it is to beat the best indoors: their only previous meeting with Zverev on this surface came at the 2024 Paris Masters, and he lost. For both of them, today is practically a final.

## The rackets of the eight masters

Three details worth chewing on:

- **Alcaraz and Fils share a frame**: both play the Pure Aero 98 with a 16x20 pattern, the control-oriented member of the Aero family. It is the only model that appears twice in the field.
- **Zverev doesn't play the racquet that is sold**. His Gravity Tour is the 100-square-inch production mould; the one that reaches shops is the **Gravity Tour 98**, with 16x19 and an RA of 61. Same silo, different tool.
- **The tensions map each player's style**: Tiafoe is the loosest in the field (PolyTour Pro 1.20 at **19 kg**), Alcaraz the tightest (RPM Team 1.30 at **25/23 kg**), and Medvedev and Fils sit at **22 kg** with very different strings (Razor Soft and RPM Blast).
