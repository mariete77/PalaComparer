The ninth edition of the Laver Cup takes place this weekend at **The O2 in London (September 25-27)**: three days, twelve matches and tennis's two "national teams" — **Europe** against the **rest of the world** — battling to be first to **13 points** (if tied after 12 matches, a deciding doubles set is played). It returns to the British capital, where Roger Federer played his final professional match in 2022, with **Yannick Noah** (Europe) and **Andre Agassi** (World) as captains, Agassi's team defending the title they won in San Francisco 2025 by a commanding **15-9**. (Sources: lavercup.com, atptour.com, Wilson, Babolat, Yonex, head.com, Tennisnerd, iq.tennis, Diadem, tennisexpress.com.)

No other tournament puts so many manufacturers on one court: London will feature **12 racquets from 5 brands** (5 HEAD, 3 Wilson, 2 Yonex, 1 Babolat and Bublik's Diadem), and five of those players use models that are already in our catalog. Let's break down the arsenal of both teams.

## Europe: Alcaraz, Zverev and the generational handover

**Carlos Alcaraz** arrives as the team's big name with the **2026 Babolat Pure Aero 98** — <ProductRef id="babolat-pure-aero-98-2026" /> —, the 98 sq-in, 16x20 frame he shares with Holger Rune and Felix Auger-Aliassime (source: Babolat). Last year in San Francisco he came away without the title despite winning his Sunday singles: **Taylor Fritz beat him for the first time** in the match that tipped the competition.

**Alexander Zverev** lands in London **12 days after lifting the US Open** (6-3 7-6(2) 5-7 6-2 vs Shelton), his second Grand Slam of the year after Roland Garros. He does so with the **Head Gravity Tour Zverev 2026** — <ProductRef id="head-gravity-tour-zverev-2026" /> —, the gold-and-purple limited edition he won both titles with. It was he who lost the deciding match of the 2025 final to Fritz: at The O2, a personal rematch awaits.

**Jakub Mensik**, who was already in San Francisco, plays with the **Wilson Blade 98 v10** — <ProductRef id="wilson-blade-98-v10-2026" /> —, the frame Wilson unveiled in April as "the most played racket on tour", also used by Sabalenka, Tsitsipas and Mirra Andreeva (source: official Wilson release). The Czech comes off losing the first rubber of the Davis Cup to Learner Tien in Prague (6-2 6-4) and will look to rediscover his best tennis in London.

The team's big newcomer is **Rafael Jodar**, who just turned 20: he leads the race for the Next Gen ATP Finals (2,509 points, more than double the second player) and sits **No. 13 in the world** after a dream 2026 — 2024 US Open boys' champion, first ATP title in Marrakech, quarter-finals in Madrid and Rome, and 27th seed at Roland Garros, where he debuted by beating Kovacevic 6-1 6-0 6-4. Jodar plays with the **HEAD Speed MP 2026** — <ProductRef id="head-speed-mp-2026" /> —, the all-rounder of the Speed line, the same family Novak Djokovic uses.

**Flavio Cobolli** rounds out the European arsenal with the **HEAD Radical** family (his actual frame is a Radical-mold pro stock; the model HEAD sells for his collection is the Radical Pro). The Italian, with a career-high of **No. 12**, returns to a Laver Cup he already played in 2025. And **Casper Ruud** stays loyal to **Yonex**: the Norwegian endorses the **EZONE 100** (in our catalog you'll find its 98 sq-in sister, Shelton and Pegula's <ProductRef id="yonex-ezone-98-2025" />), with a tour mold from the DR 100 family.

## Rest of the World: Fritz, De Minaur's Ultra and Bublik's Diadem

**Taylor Fritz** is the man who gave Agassi's team the title in San Francisco: first he beat then-No. 1 Alcaraz — his first career win over him — and then sealed the trophy by winning the last match against Zverev (official Laver Cup report). He plays with the **HEAD Radical MP** — <ProductRef id="head-radical-mp-auxetic" /> —, the same family as Andrey Rublev's, already in the catalog; note that his actual frame is an IG-era Radical MP with an 18x20 pattern (pro stock TGT 260.3) dressed in new paint. This year he fell in the third round of the US Open to Francisco Cerundolo, having been two sets up.

**Alex de Minaur**, Saturday's hero in San Francisco (his win over Zverev tilted the final), plays with the **Wilson Ultra Pro 99** — one of the in-vogue racquets on tour, with the Ultra family also in Learner Tien's hands (source: Tennisnerd). That makes **Wilson the only brand with players on both teams**... with a curious caveat: **Tien's** racquet is an **unreleased, non-retail mold** (confirmed by Tennisnerd), so neither his frame nor De Minaur's has a direct store equivalent.

**Brandon Nakashima**, 2022 Next Gen ATP Finals champion and this year's National Bank Open finalist in Toronto, makes his Laver Cup debut with the **Yonex VCORE 98** — <ProductRef id="yonex-vcore-98-2026" /> —, the spin-and-control frame he shares with Hubert Hurkacz (sources: iq.tennis, Tennisnerd).

The tournament's curiosity is **Alexander Bublik**: the Kazakh, **No. 16 in the world** with a ceiling of No. 10 in January 2026, is the **only player on the roster not using a big-brand racquet**. Since this year he plays with the **Diadem Project Bublik 98**, his signature frame developed with US brand Diadem — which boasts that a top-20 player competes with a racquet "you can buy" (source: diademsports.com). He makes his debut in the competition.

And Fritz's New York executioner, **Francisco Cerundolo**, carries one of the most seasoned racquets in the locker room: a **HEAD Prestige pro stock** (PT57A mold, 18x20 pattern, around 350 g strung per Tennisnerd), the same control family HEAD has equipped him with since childhood (head.com). Cerundolo already knows what it's like to win in this competition: in 2025 he toppled Holger Rune on Saturday.
