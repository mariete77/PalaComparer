The **China Open in Beijing** (an ATP 500 running from September 30 to October 6 at the National Tennis Center) is heading into its final stretch with the biggest tennis story of the week: **Novak Djokovic, at 39, is still undefeated in the Chinese capital and now owns the best start to a single tournament in Open Era history.** His victim yesterday was the top seed and double Grand Slam champion of 2026, Alexander Zverev.

## Djokovic d. Zverev: Nadal's record falls in Beijing

The Serb came from behind to beat the German **4-6, 6-4, 6-4 in two hours and 35 minutes**, with Zverev firing 19 aces, in their first meeting since Roland Garros 2025 — with the German having claimed Paris and New York since then. The match had its moment of controversy: an LED advertising board went dark during a rally, Zverev asked for a video review and the chair umpire ordered the point to be replayed, much to Djokovic's frustration.

"I had a terrible start. Two double faults at the beginning. It was quite cold and, honestly, I didn't feel warmed up, but my fault," the Serb admitted. "I am just glad that I held my nerves, played good shots at important moments, 5-4 in the second and third."

With the win, Djokovic moves to **32-0 in Beijing**, passing **Rafael Nadal** (31-0 at Roland Garros) for the best start to a single event by a men's player in the Open Era. The historical table is completed by McEnroe (23-0 in Milan) and Wilander (23-0 in Barcelona). The Belgrade native is a six-time champion in the city, is playing there for the first time since 2015 — when he beat Nadal in the final for title number six — and is two wins away from a seventh... and from his first trophy of 2026. Along the way, he denied Zverev a golden chance to gain ground in the race for world No. 1.

## The other quarters: Medvedev rolls on, Rublev and Khachanov out

- **Daniil Medvedev (3)** dismissed Francisco Cerúndolo **7-6(4), 6-3** and stretched his winning streak to **seven matches**, without dropping a set all tournament (he beat Carreño Busta 6-4 6-2 and Struff 6-4 6-3 in the earlier rounds).
- **Alex de Minaur (5)** came from behind to beat Andrey Rublev **1-6, 6-2, 6-3** in the direct clash of Nitto ATP Finals hopefuls.
- **Hubert Hurkacz** edged Karen Khachanov **7-6, 6-2**: the Pole got the reward nobody wanted, because Khachanov was precisely the upset man of the first round — the one who knocked out second seed Auger-Aliassime on his debut.
