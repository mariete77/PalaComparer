The **Premier Padel Finals** field is not decided in one go. It fills up tournament by tournament, match by match, and in Acapulco the list has just jumped forward. With the **GNP Mexico Major** round of 32 done, **Jon Sanz, Paquito Navarro, Martín Di Nenno and Fran Guerrero** already hold tickets for the **Palau Sant Jordi (10-13 December)**.

**Four places** are left in the men's draw, and a few players walk into Thursday's round of 16 with their whole season on the line. This is how the Race moved.

## Wednesday's four tickets

The first to lock it in was **Jon Sanz**. He and **Franco Stupaczuk** (3) dispatched **Tolito Aguirre and Pablo Lijó** **7-5, 6-2**, and those points mean the Navarrese no longer needs anyone else's help to be in Barcelona.

**Paquito Navarro and Martín Di Nenno** (4) followed with a win that was more comfortable than the scoreline suggested on paper: **6-4, 6-3** against **Álvaro Montiel and Daniel Santigosa**. Both arrived in Acapulco with the calculator out; both leave with the place signed.

The fourth was **Fran Guerrero**, who alongside **Javi Leal** (6) beat **Manuel Castaño and Pol Hernández** **6-4, 6-2**. That is where the story splits: **Guerrero is in, Leal is not**. Guerrero's partner sits **73 points off the last qualifying place** and needed the round of 16 badly.

## Javi Leal and Tino Libaak: 73 and 75 points

The numbers Premier Padel published before the event leave almost no margin. The last spot stood at **3,310 points**, held by **Momo González** in sixteenth. **Javi Leal** arrived with **3,237**; **Tino Libaak** with **3,235**. Seventy-three and seventy-five points — one round-of-16 match.

Both faced the same problem: neither draw was a gift.

- **Leal-Guerrero (6) against Edu Alonso and Aimar Goñi (10)**, direct rivals for the same place. **Edu Alonso** came in on **3,225 points**, twenty-first, 85 behind the cut.
- **Libaak-Alfonso (9) against Stupaczuk and Sanz (3)** — a pair whose Sanz half already has the ticket in his pocket and plays without scoreboard pressure.

**Gonza Alfonso** is the strangest case of all: he holds one of the cut-off places (fifteenth, **3,325 points**) while his partner Libaak is outside. A pair competing as one for a ticket that today only one of them owns.

## Arce and Tello wait on others

The other side of the day belonged to **Maxi Arce and Juan Tello** (7). They came in thirteenth on **3,715 points**, the best-placed pair of everyone fighting for a place, and lost to **Ramiro Valenzuela and Xisco Gil** **6(5)-7, 5-7**. The defeat does not eliminate them mathematically, but it means they no longer control their own fate.

The pair who beat them is still alive. **Valenzuela and Gil**, who had already come from behind against Barahona and Piotto, meet **Marc Sintes and Coquito Zamora** on Thursday; Sintes and Zamora needed three sets (**6-2, 6(4)-7, 6-3**) to get past **Javi Garrido and Juani de Pascual**. Two unseeded pairs, one match from a Major quarter-final.

## What Thursday decides

The round of 16 is played on Thursday night in Acapulco — early hours of Friday in Spain — across four courts. The men's matches, and what is at stake:

On the women's side the top four seeds enter directly in the round of 16, and the other big count of the week lives there: **Triay and Brea** need to reach the final to seal the No. 1 for a second straight year. **Ari Sánchez and Ustero**, their chasers, must win both the Major and the Finals and hope for a slip.

## The rackets of those already in

Three of the four qualifiers have a page in our catalogue, and the split says a lot about how each one plays.

**Jon Sanz** is the only one of the three on a **teardrop**: the <ProductRef id="drop-shot-axion-attack-1-0-2025" />, 360-375 grams, medium balance, EVA Soft core and 24K carbon faces. It is the racket of a right-side player who builds from the back and does not need a diamond to finish the point.

**Paquito Navarro** sits at the opposite end: the <ProductRef id="bullpadel-hack-05-2027" />, a diamond with TriCarbon 18K faces, high balance at 26.5 cm and 522 cm². The tenth-anniversary Hack is pure dynamic power, and Paquito still picks the most demanding frame in the range.

And **Martín Di Nenno** has moved to the <ProductRef id="bullpadel-xplo-02-2027" />: Bullpadel's most extreme XPLO, a 38 mm geometric head, balance close to 27 cm and only 507 cm² of surface. Less face, more mass behind the ball. For the right-side player in an attacking pair, that is a statement.

Among those playing for their lives tonight, **Tino Libaak** is the exception: the <ProductRef id="siux-diablo-pro-2026" /> is a **hybrid**, 18K TeXtreme over 3K, with a sweet spot that survives off-centre contact. **Edu Alonso**, his direct rival in the Race, swings the <ProductRef id="nox-ea10-ventus-attack-12k-2026" />, a diamond with a firm feel and a very live exit. And **Juan Tello**, who no longer controls his own fate, plays the <ProductRef id="bullpadel-vertex-05-2027" />, the 530 cm² diamond with which Bullpadel has brought swingweight down from the previous generation.
